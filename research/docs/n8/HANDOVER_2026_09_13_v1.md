## 1. What the construction is

Eight runners, distinct constant speeds, circle of circumference 1, threshold 1/8. Seven parts.
Six are proved with the proofs written out; the seventh is the claim.

| | part | uses | does **not** use |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Loneliness is a two-neighbour condition; there are no end runners | the cyclic order on the track | measure, density, any set of "bad" times |
| 2(a) | All eight lonely ⟺ every gap is exactly 1/8 | `Σ gaps = 1` | anything |
| 2(b) | The instants are `{δt} = r/n`, `gcd(r,n)=1`; there are `φ(n)` of them | `lrc_ap_phi_n_law_v1` Thm 3.1(a)(b) — owner's own paper | Prop 4.1 of that paper (a sketch), and any measure statement |
| 3 | `d_min` from the two neighbours is exact; nothing happens before `1/(8·d_min)` | sortedness of the speeds | any global property of the arrangement |
| 4 | One window: `u ∈ [1/8, 7/8]` in the lap coordinate | part 3 | intervals of "bad" time, complements |
| 5 | Every other runner's lap count and phase at every `u` | arithmetic | search, sampling, approximation |
| 6 | The reference instant, and what a deviation does to it | the permutation `k ↦ kr (mod n)` | measure-zero arguments, stability radii |
| 7 | **The claim**: the procedure returns the moment | — | — |

**The whole construction has exactly one external debt**, and it is the owner's own prior work:
`lrc_ap_phi_n_law_v1` Theorem 3.1(a)–(b), used in part 2(b).

## 2. The three quantities the construction runs on

Everything is expressed in these, and nothing else.

- **Gaps.** The eight cyclic gaps on the track, summing to 1. A runner is lonely when its two
  flanking gaps are each `≥ 1/8`.
- **`d_min`.** Each runner's nearest speed difference, read from its two neighbours. Exact,
  local, and unremovable — sortedness cannot be deviated away.
- **Laps.** In the coordinate `u = d_min · t`, runner `j` has completed `⌊s_j u⌋ ` laps relative to
  the runner in question and sits at phase `s_j u mod 1`. It is clear when that phase is between
  `1/8` and `7/8`.

Part 6's displacement is stated in these terms too: at a reference instant, a deviation `e_k`
moves runner `k` by `e_k · r / d` **eighths of the circle**. ("Slot" was a word of mine for an
eighth; the fact does not need it and v3's successor should not use it.)

## 3. What part 6 says, in full

At `t = r/(nd)` with `gcd(r,n) = 1`, writing `V = A + e` where `A` is an **arithmetic
progression** of common difference `d` (an AP — not the same thing as a grid; see §5):

- the AP part places the `n` runners on the `n` distinct eighths, one each, **for every runner at
  once** — which is why the reference gives global isolation and needs no runner held still;
- the deviation moves runner `k` by exactly `e_k r / d` eighths `[PROVED]`;
- **the instant survives iff every `e_k r/d` is a whole number of eighths and
  `k ↦ kr + e_k r/d (mod n)` is a bijection** `[PROVED]`, checked 12,135 / 12,135.

The last of these replaced a citation to Proposition 4.1 of the φ(n) paper. That was the right
trade twice over: Prop 4.1 is a *Proof Sketch* in its source, and it is a measure statement, which
this construction does not use anywhere.

It also corrected a wrong guess of mine. Not every deviation destroys the instant: displacements
that **permute** the eighths preserve it. `e = (0,0,8,2,0,−2,0,8)` at `r = 7` leaves all eight
runners lonely.

## 4. The claim, and what is known about it

> **Claim.** For every arrangement and every runner, the procedure — `d_min` from the two
> neighbours, the window, read the other six inside it — **returns the moment**.

It is stated as a property of the procedure. It contains no existential quantifier, no witness, no
set, no complement.

**Already proved**, from parts 1–6 alone:

| case | coverage |
|---|---|
| `d_max ≤ 7·d_min` | 35,467 / 35,467 |
| every grid and every dilate | 485 / 485 |
| any runner with no `s_j` within 1 of a multiple of 8 | ≈ 49.5% of runners |
| the same at `u = r/8`, `r ∈ {1,3,5,7}` | more than the above |

**Checked where not proved:** 21,600 runner-instances across seven populations, and exhaustively
every integer arrangement with `0 = v_1 < … < v_8 ≤ 20` and gcd 1 — 77,400 arrangements, 113,145
distinct profiles, no failure to return.

**LRC(8) is not proved by this document.**

## 5. Traps, each of which was hit at least once

1. **BSM is mandatory.** The AP's own reading is **four isolated points**, not an interval. Any
   implementation that merges touching closed zones, or evaluates cell midpoints only, reports the
   AP's end runners as never lonely — and compiles, and looks right. Evaluate boundary points and
   open-cell midpoints separately. This was hit while building the procedure in §2 of
   `OWNER_PROCEDURE_v1`.

2. **Grid ⊋ AP.** A grid need not have a common difference: `(1,2,3,4,5,6,15,16)` is a grid and is
   not an AP. Part 6 decomposes against an **AP**; part 2 is about **grids**. v2 conflated them.

3. **The second neighbour is not free.** `NEIGHBOUR_INTERVAL_v1` §1 step 3 says "verify the other
   five". It must be **six**: `d_min` comes from the nearer neighbour, and the other one has to be
   read like everyone else. Counterexample `V = (4,12,29,37,101,121,176,196)`, runner r4 — the
   second neighbour sits exactly on its lap line at `u = 1/8` while all five non-neighbours are
   clear; five-only returns `1/8`, the truth is `9/64`. Differs in 3,276 of 48,000.
   **`NEIGHBOUR_INTERVAL_v1` still needs this correction.**

4. **Measure-zero is the AP's signature, not a difficulty.** Runners whose reading is four
   isolated points are seeing an AP — possibly based elsewhere. In `(8,9,10,11,15,16,17,18)`,
   runners r4 and r5 have differences `1,2,3,4,5,6,7`: pure APs based at 11 and 15. They are the
   *easy* cases.
