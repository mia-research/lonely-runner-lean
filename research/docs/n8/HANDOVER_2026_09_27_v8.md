## 3. What the cascade taught (mechanism, E119–E130)

- **The cascade, literally:** each reading blocks open windows `((8k−1)/s, (8k+1)/s)` of `R`; the cascade walks right
  through the **union of windows** and lands at its first gap. In log coordinates the clear places form a fixed comb
  `[0, log 7] ∪ [log 9, log 15] ∪ …` (the grid is infinite) and changing the instant slides the readings rigidly (the AP
  is scale invariant). A chain of windows needs, at each junction `(a,k)→(b,l)`, `s_b/s_a > (8l−1)/(8k+1)`.
- **The band's two copies:** the nearest runner's window `(7, 9)` splits `[1, 15]` into copy 0 `[1, 7]` and copy 1
  `[9, 15]`; from copy 0 to copy 1 every runner's window lattice slides by the fraction `{sᵢ}` of its period. Late events
  (copy 0 fully covered) exist only for fine shapes; the supremum shape `≈ (1, 2.35, 3.24, 4.1886, 5.2223, 5.7871, 7.4378)`
  is lonely in copy 0 at a single pinch point (`R = 6.319`), killed by perturbation, isolation jumping to `≈ 12`.
- **Crowds:** adjacent readings of ratio `ρ` share clear blocks only on laps `k ≤ (7 − ρ)/(8(ρ − 1))`; every late landing
  comes after the first run-out; after it the cascade splits the crowd at an internal gap (two-block inside the crowd).
  At lap `k` a window's relative width is `≈ 1 + 1/(4k)`: any two distinct readings are eventually separable; only equal
  speeds never are (the owner's *"two runners at one speed"*).
- **The owner's reference, found in the data (E128–E129):** every band set to 24 (42,811) is lonely at a relocated
  **`φ(8)` class instant** (`c ∈ {1, 3, 5, 7}`) of a **grid through one of its own readings at scale `sⱼ` or `sⱼ/2`**
  (`m ≤ 2`), decided exactly by the pairwise theorem (`redo/grid_class_control_v1.out`; the control shows this is not
  the "any flank opening" tautology). The band to 32 (365,287) and all late shapes: `m ≤ 2`. Case B to 24: `m ≤ 4` (with
  `c` unrestricted; re-check with `c ≤ 7` owed). What decides a class: interior readings drift, the two edge readings
  need a sign, mirror classes swap the edges (the pair law, `grid_pair_law`). The small-deviation criterion
  (`grid_class_clear`) covers 90% of the band to 24; the rest need the exact (Helly) form.

## 4. The open step, stated exactly

**Replace Case B's certificates by a derivation of:** *for every set of spread in `(7, 24]`, some reading `j`, some
`m ≤ 4` and some class `c ∈ {1, 3, 5, 7}` give a pairwise-compatible lap assignment.* Each such assignment is already a
proved rule (`lonely_eight_of_pairwise_laps`); what is not derived is that one always exists. The pair law
(`grid_pair_law`) is its first piece: it closes the case of one reading per edge with small deviation. Still to derive:
(i) edges holding two readings (the doubled slot at an edge); (ii) deviations beyond the pair law's `1/C` (answered in
the data by the finer grid `m = 2`). **The `m = 2` exceptions** (E130, `redo/grid_class_m2_exceptions_v1.out`): 73 band
sets to 24; they are grids at half the nearest runner's scale (half-integer shapes such as `(1, 1.5, 5.5, 7, 7.5, 8, 9)`,
and the AP with one position moved, `(1, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8)`); single `±1` moves restore `m = 1` in 60–95 ways per rank,
so `m = 2` is a knife-edge. Reading: the finer grid is the arrangement's own natural unit (AP scale invariance chooses
the scale at which the arrangement is a grid).

Routes tried and why each is not the proof (do not re-derive): fixed-rank flanks (989 band sets uncovered); the AP's
classes matched by rank (18%); cascade paths (2,141 and growing); merged places at the landing (57% have seven distinct);
the top reading's gaps `G₁`, `G₂` (both coverable); phase-free nesting (dies in two steps on dense readings); the
cover's Farkas certificates read as cycles (not canonical).
