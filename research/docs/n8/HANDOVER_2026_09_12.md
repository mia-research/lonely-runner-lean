## 1. The owner's framework, stated correctly

This took most of the session to state right. It is precise, and it was precise from the
beginning.

**A grid is an AP with elements missing.** Not "an AP with periodic deletion". Any set whose
reduced residues form a complete system mod `n`, where **`n` is the number of runners**.
`(2,4,6,104)` is a grid: residues mod 4 are `{0,1,2,3}`, complete, and it is globally isolated
at `t = 1/8` (`g = 2`, so instants are `p/(ng) = p/8`). `(1,2,3,8)` likewise. Spread is irrelevant — it lives entirely in the deviation
part `q` of `u_i = π(i) + n q_i`, which global isolation cannot see.

**The modulus is `n`, and it is forced — not a convention.** `n` runners make `n` gaps summing
to 1; if every gap is `≥ 1/n` then every gap is exactly `1/n`, or the sum exceeds 1. So the
positions must be the uniform `n`-grid and the criterion is a complete residue system mod `n`.

> **CORRECTION (see §2a).** An earlier version of this section said the modulus "belongs to
> the AP, not to `n`", and gave `(2,6,7,8,10,13,14)` as a grid achieving global isolation.
> **Both are wrong.** Being drawn from the AP `1..14` does not confer modulus 14; that set has
> seven runners, so its modulus is 7, its residues `{0,1,4,5,6}` are incomplete, and its best
> min-gap is `1/9 < 1/7` — it is **not** globally isolated. The "mod 14" remark in the source
> conversation was about the AP `1..14` itself, which has fourteen runners.

**There is no stationary runner.** Translating so one runner sits at 0 is free and changes
nothing. *Adjoining* a runner at 0 changes `n` and the whole question. `(2,4,6,104)` as four
runners achieves global isolation; as five it does not. This was baked into memory
mid-session after being got wrong repeatedly.

**Runners pass each other.** They keep going round the track. "Collisions" are not events.

**Global isolation ≠ LRC.** A grid puts every runner lonely at the *same* instant. LRC needs
each runner lonely at *some* instant. The grid is a reference point, not the target.

**The programme.** Every arrangement is a permutation/deviation from an AP or grid. Map what
permutations do to individual isolation. The hypothesis: *"there will be a low point of
individual isolation points but it can't escape before getting better."*

## 3. The owner's calls that were right

Recorded because they were right against my resistance, and because the pattern is
informative.

- **Grid = AP with gaps, at any spread.** Verified: global isolation ⟺ complete residue
  system mod `n`, and the deviations are invisible to it. `(2,4,6,104)` and `(1,2,3,8)` are
  the correct worked examples.
- **Translation vs adjoining.**
- **Transit mapping** — vary a runner's speed toward its neighbour and map the effect on every
  runner. This produced the directional law, the recovery law, and the reserve accounting.
- **The palindrome.** *"Isolation happens in that palindrome fashion, so there are several
  witnessing opportunities."* `G` is symmetric under `t → 1−t`; components pair; count 1 is
  impossible whenever any constraint is even.
- **Recovery.** *"One move takes away an isolation, movement of the others will start to give
  it back."* Confirmed exactly: the victim recovers in 81% of arrangements after one further
  move, 96.3% after seven.
- **The grid is the spread option.** I had written that nothing joined the "dense" and
  "spread" regions; the grid work was that join.
- **Measure, not modulus.** *"You measure resolution which also behaves predictably"* — led to
  the measure/component-count distinction and eventually to the popcorn structure.
- **Interaction.** *"Not just how individual runners affect the others but how they
  interact"* — led to `S(t)`, `max S`, and the maximal-set system.

## 4. Where an intuition needed refinement

One case, recorded for completeness. Early on: *"if you move runner off 1/8, the rest of the
runners can't still be reaching individual isolation at the grid points."*

They can. A runner keeps all four `p/8` instants unless one of **its own** differences becomes
`≡ 0 mod 8`. Moving one runner therefore costs the spine to exactly the **two** runners that
now collide mod 8, and leaves the other six untouched — usually improving them. This is the
spine mechanism (`SPINE_AND_RESERVE_v1`) and it is sharper than either of us expected.

## 5. What the framework actually yields

**Proved.** The `φ(8)` spine and its all-or-nothing property; the strict nesting of the
eighth-lattice; `max S = 8 ⟺ grid`; `max S ≥ spine count`; `k ≤ 6`; the slot-counting bound;
count 1 impossible with any even constraint; `count ≥ (4·max D/3)·|G_D|`.

**The central identity.** `base min = 4 + (fewest extras)` — exact over 6,305 grid shapes. A
grid gives every runner the spine; shapes differ only in **reserve**. The tight AP is the
unique worst grid because its two end runners have zero reserve.

**What a deviation does.** It removes the spine from exactly the colliding pair, leaves the
reserve statistically unchanged (mean 5.84 → 5.87), and the crashed runner lands on a finer
2-power lattice.

**Where the danger is.** Exactly one residue collision: min count by number of collisions runs
4, **2**, 4, 8. Grid distance `k = 1..4`, with both flanks safe for different reasons.

**Why the two premises do not close it.** If some configuration had a runner at count 0, any
path from the reference has a *first* move breaking the invariant. So "the invariant survives
every move" is logically identical to "no configuration has count 0". The framework re-indexes
n = 8 by move rather than by configuration. This is the single most important structural fact
in the session and it is not a defect of the idea — it is what any argument of this shape must
overcome.

## 6. Failure modes with a track record here

Nine retractions, none from arithmetic. All from design:

- a bound read off a region the search could not reach (the `mmin` ceiling of 29; the
  floor-versus-span table; "low counts only at small span");
- a pattern generalised from one example ("the adversary chooses halves"; "crashed runners land
  on sixteenths"; "the worst permutation reconstitutes an AP");
- quantities compared on different scales (count vs rate; scaled vs original time — **twice**);
- a proxy adopted without validating it (`mmin`, and earlier the `K` statistic).

**Exact arithmetic and BSM conformance protect the numbers and nothing else.** Three
implementations agree with 0 disagreements over 495 sets and 0 BSM cell anomalies; every error
above survived that.
