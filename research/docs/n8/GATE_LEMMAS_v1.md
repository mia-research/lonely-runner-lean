# The gate lemmas, with proofs (v1, 2026-10-04; fresh-context adversarial review done, repairs applied)

Objects only from the cascade: readings 1 = ρ_1 < ρ_2 < ... < ρ_7 (speed minus the subject's, units of d_min; all positive and distinct); zones Z(y,j) = ((8j−1)/ρ_y, (8j+1)/ρ_y), j ≥ 1, open, pairwise disjoint for one reading, each of width 2/ρ_y; a point R ≥ 1 is *blocked* if it lies in some zone; the cascade from R = 1 jumps from a blocked state to the largest exit among the zones containing it; the *landing* R* is the first unblocked R ≥ 1 (the least unblocked point, because every point passed over lies inside a used zone). Reading 1's zones are (7,9), (15,17), ...; R ∈ [1,7] is never blocked by reading 1 (its j = 0 zone ends at 1).

Carving: K_1 = [1,7]; K_k = K_{k−1} minus the zones of reading k (k = 2..7). A *window* of K_k is a connected component (a closed interval; one point allowed). K_7 is the set of lonely windows in [1,7]; the order of the readings does not change K_7.

Status of each statement: P = proved here by hand (no computation used in the proof); T = tested exactly on the stated sets (a check, not part of the proof); L = in Lean (`ZoneLinkN`).

## L1 (the gate) [(a), (b), (c) P; (d) T only]
(a) R* > 7 ⇔ K_7 = ∅; no landing lies in (7,9). (b) If R* > 7 the path has a state in (7,9). (c) At the first such state s_0, reading 1 is a blocker with exit 9; it is the setter unless some other zone containing s_0 also contains 9 (exit > 9). (d) OBSERVED, not proved: the move from s_0 always lands at exactly 9 (reading 1's zone is the setter; `path()` breaks an exact tie of exits toward the larger index, so "reading 1 is the setter" is convention-dependent, "the move from s_0 lands at 9" is not), so "R* > 7 ⇔ a state equals 9": 0 exceptions among 15,203 crossing members (J=1 7,567, J=2 7,636) and the 12,078 crossing tuples of E366.
*Proof of (a)–(c).* (a) R* ≤ 7 ⇔ some point of [1,7] is unblocked ⇔ K_7 ≠ ∅, because reading 1 does not block [1,7]. If K_7 = ∅, every point of (7,9) is blocked by reading 1's zone (7,9), so R* ∉ (7,9) and R* ≥ 9. (b) A zone of a reading y ≥ 2 has width 2/ρ_y < 2, so a jump from a state s ≤ 7 lands at an exit < s + 2 ≤ 9; the first state above 7 therefore lies in (7,9). (c) At s_0 the zone (7,9) of reading 1 has exit 9; another blocker's exit exceeds 9 only if its zone contains s_0 and 9, i.e. the reading blocks R = 9. ∎
*Gap (d).* A violation needs a reading y ≥ 2 with a zone (L,U) containing both s_0 and 9 (L < s_0, U > 9); the previous state s ≤ 7 then satisfies L ≥ s (else y would have been the setter at s), and the setter z before s_0 has its exit in (L, 9) and its entry ≤ 7, which forces ρ_z in about (1, 1.56) and ρ_y near 1.7-1.9: two readings close to d_min. A reading y ≥ 2 that blocks R = 9 and whose zone contains the first state above 7 would be the setter instead of reading 1, and reading 1 would never set a move although R* > 7. No argument excludes this; the exact searches (members, seeds, 3.6 million constructed arrangements, a directed annealing on the violation structure whose minimum clear length was 0.0788 and never 0, a 259,123-step random walk inside the crossing set with the move from s_0 landing at 9 every time, and 119,997 random arrangements with a zone straddling 9, none of which crossed) found none; in every crossing step the zones containing 9 start at least 0.86 beyond s_0; and the complete tree (all 1,878,060 outcome regions with rho_7 <= 25, float-LP): of the 7,947 regions whose whole landing range lies above 7, 7,946 contain reading 1's move at lap 1 and the one without it is a degenerate region whose landing range is the single value 7.000000000000001 (a landing at exactly 7, not above 7). The statement E340 graded [PROVED] was proved only as (a)-(c).

## L2 (one window per zone) [P; T pending (`one_window_per_zone_v1.out`)]
For k = 2..7, a zone of reading k contains at most one window of K_{k−1}.
*Proof.* Suppose two consecutive windows W_1 = [a_1,b_1] < W_2 = [a_2,b_2] of K_{k−1} lie in one zone Z of reading k. The gap (b_1,a_2) is non-empty and contained in Z, so its length is at most |Z| = 2/ρ_k. A point x of the gap is not in K_{k−1} but lies in [1,7], so it is blocked by a zone V of some reading y < k. The endpoints b_1 and a_2 belong to K_{k−1}, so V contains neither; V is an interval containing x, hence V ⊂ (b_1,a_2). Then 2/ρ_y = |V| ≤ |Z| = 2/ρ_k, i.e. ρ_y ≥ ρ_k, contradicting y < k. ∎

## L3 (reading 7 crosses every window of K_6; gate crossers) [P; T 12,078/12,078]
Suppose K_7 = ∅ (R* > 7). Then each window of K_6 lies inside exactly one zone of reading 7 (existence: a window point is blocked, not by readings 1..6, so by reading 7; uniqueness: connected, zones disjoint), the windows lie in pairwise distinct zones (L2), and each such zone is a setter zone (a move) of the path before the gate.
*Proof of the last claim.* The used zones of the path cover [1, R*) (each used zone contains the previous exit; the union is connected from 1). A window point lies in a used zone; the only zones meeting K_6 belong to reading 7; so the zone of reading 7 holding the window is a used zone, i.e. a move. ∎
*Corollary.* A gate crosser's path has at least n_6 moves by reading 7, where n_6 is the number of windows of K_6. Moreover ρ_7 < 2/(length of the longest window of K_6).

## L4 (closer; K2s1 = at least two moves, opener on lap 1, closer the opener's own reading) [P; T 1,593/1,593]
Let R* be a landing with window [R*, R* + w], opener the zone (s,j) of reading s whose exit is R*, and for each reading y let p_y = R*ρ_y mod 8 ∈ [1,7] be its position at R* (p_s = 1). The first entry after R* is that of the reading minimising (7 − p_y)/ρ_y. The opener's own reading is that minimiser only if s = 7.
*Proof.* A reading y with ρ_y > ρ_s has (7 − p_y)/ρ_y ≤ 6/ρ_y < 6/ρ_s = (7 − p_s)/ρ_s. ∎ Hence K2s1 (≥ 2 moves, opener lap 1, closer the opener's reading) is empty: the opener would be rank 7 on lap 1, so a previous setter p slower than 7 on lap 1 would need ρ_p > ρ_7, and on lap ≥ 2 an exit 17/ρ_p < 9/ρ_7, i.e. ρ_p > (17/9)ρ_7; neither holds.

## L5 (the staircase) [P; T 119,278 members and 400,000 random per window]
(a) Each move multiplies the state by at most (8l+1)/(8l−1) ≤ 9/7 (the state lies inside the zone, hence beyond its entry (8l−1)/ρ, and the new state is its exit (8l+1)/ρ). (b) The setter of the move from state R_k has ρ > 7/R_k. (c) Consecutive setters differ (the setter is at position 1 at its exit). (d) If the first k+1 setters are distinct then ρ_{7−k} > 7(7/9)^k.
*Proof.* (a) as stated. (b) zone entry ≥ 7/ρ < R_k. (c) clear. (d) the i-th setter has ρ > 7/R_{i−1} > 7(7/9)^{i−1} by (a) and R_{i−1} < (9/7)^{i−1}; the k+1 distinct setters have ranks ≤ 7 − k at their lowest, so ρ_{7−k} ≥ that setter's reading. ∎

## L6 (link rule) [L, `ZoneLinkN`; T 136,199]
After a move to the exit of (s,j), a slower reading blocks only at a lap ≤ j and a faster one only at a lap ≥ j+1; equal readings cannot link.

## L7 (the crossing family is open and its boundary consists of touches) [P; consistent with the adjacency search: 28,511 of 28,511 observed losses]
Call an arrangement a *crosser* if K_7 = ∅ (L1(a)). Let ρ(s), s ∈ [0,1], be a continuous path of arrangements (readings continuous, order kept), crossing for s < s* and not crossing at s*. Then at s* the set K_7(s*) is a non-empty finite set of points, and each of its points x is a *touch*: x is the exit of one zone and the entry of another, the two zones belonging to readings (any of 1..7) with reading 1's zones included (its zone (7,9) has entry 7; its lap-0 zone ends at 1). In particular the crossing arrangements form an open set whose boundary consists of arrangements with an exit-equals-entry coincidence (a rational ratio of two readings: (8j+1) ρ_a = (8j'−1) ρ_b).
*Proof.* K_7(s*) ≠ ∅ by L1(a). Let x ∈ K_7(s*): for s < s* x is blocked by an open zone Z(s) of some reading, and at s* it is in no zone. Zones move continuously and finitely many matter, so x is within a distance that tends to 0 of an endpoint of a zone at s*; if K_7(s*) contained an interval of positive length, every point of it would be within that distance of the finitely many zone endpoints, which fails once the distance is below the interval's half-length; hence K_7(s*) is finite. For an isolated point x of the clear set at s*, points just left of x are blocked, by a zone whose right end is then x (an exit), and points just right of x are blocked, by a zone whose left end is x (an entry); at x = 1 the left neighbour is covered by reading 1's lap-0 zone (exit 1), at x = 7 the right neighbour by reading 1's zone (7,9) (entry 7). ∎
*Consequence for the catalogue.* The set of crossing arrangements is a union of open cells of the hyperplane arrangement formed by the touch hyperplanes (8j+1) ρ_a = (8j'−1) ρ_b (and the corresponding rank and setter rows). It is a finite union exactly when only finitely many touch hyperplanes bound it, i.e. when the laps j, j' that occur are bounded. The adjacency search (E373) has so far met 143 distinct hyperplanes among the 28,511 crossing losses; the complete tree (E368) has 7,947 crossing regions up to rho_7 = 25.

## Consequences that follow from L1-L6 and an observation, not from them
The opener of a gate-crossed outcome has R* = (8j+1)/ρ_s with R* ≥ 9 [proved, L1] and R* < 15 [observed: the maximum found is 11.966; < 15 is d_min's window end and is itself unproved] and lap j = (ρ_s R* − 1)/8, so the confinement of the B opener laps (rank 2: 3-5, 3: 4-6, 4: 5-6, 5: 5-8, 6: 7-9, 7: 8-18 at ρ_7 ≤ 25) is an exact consequence of things not proved here: upper AND lower bounds on the readings of crossers (observed upper bounds ρ_6 ≤ 8.14, ρ_5 ≤ 7.07, ρ_4 ≤ 6.19, ρ_3 ≤ 5.28, ρ_2 ≤ 4.76; ρ_7 up to 24.38; observed lower bounds ρ_2 ≥ 2.16, ρ_3 ≥ 2.94, ρ_4 ≥ 3.83, ρ_5 ≥ 4.32, ρ_6 ≥ 5.45; ρ_7 > 7 is proved because some reading must block R = 1) and R* < 12 (observed 11.966 maximum). The 14 outcome classes (opener rank × window, minus "opener 1 in window A", plus no move) are every logically possible combination, so their number is not evidence of anything by itself.
