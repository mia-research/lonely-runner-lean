## 5. NEXT: the `8 | v` collapse to `ℤ/163`

The `p = 163` attempt produced a framing correction that supersedes §4's
emphasis: **covering is not the obstruction — the gcd condition is.**

At `k=6, p=31` (Lemma 7 applies) the set `{7,14,21,28,35,42}` covers `{1,…,108}`
completely; it dies on `gcd`, all being multiples of 7 with `N = 7·31`. At
`k=7, p=163` likewise: `{8,32,40,48,56,88,104}` covers `{1,…,652}` completely,
all multiples of 8.

At `p = 163` the gcd condition has a closed form. `N = 1304 = 2³·163`, and `163`
never divides a whole subset, so:

> `gcd(R ∪ {N}) = 1` for every 6-subset ⟺ **`T` contains at least two odd speeds.**

The verification is exactly: *no 7 speeds in `{1,…,652} \ 163ℤ`, at least two
odd, cover `{1,…,652}` under `j·v mod 1304 ∈ [0,163) ∪ (1141,1304)`.*

Odd speeds are **not** weaker — coverage size is 162 (odd) vs 164 (`8|v`), and
counting still fails at 1.75× slack. The asymmetry is structural:

> For `8 | v`, `j·v mod 1304 = 8·(j·(v/8) mod 163)`, so an all-multiples-of-8
> configuration **collapses to covering `ℤ/163` by dilates of a 41-element arc** —
> 163 residues instead of 652 targets. Odd speeds do not collapse; they see the
> full `ℤ/1304`.

**That collapse, and why two odd speeds break it, is the next thing to examine.**

(`ℤ/163`, arc of size 41), and it is the only route not yet closed.
