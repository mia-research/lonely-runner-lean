## 2. Contributions

**2.1 The whole `n = 8` Spec layer at general `n`** — `LRC/Spec/DoorsN.lean`, 94 declarations.
A name-by-name diff shows **all 93** `n = 8` declarations present; axiom checks clean.

**2.2 The prime theorem, both halves.** At prime `n`, a subject none of whose non-flank
disparities is divisible by `n` has a lonely opening at its flank —
* `n ∤ e`: `a = e·s` with `e·s ≡ 1 (mod n)`;
* `n ∣ e`: `a = k·e + 1`, `k` from a pigeonhole on `(ℤ/n)*`.
**And the reason composite orders differ is Euclid's lemma**: `n ∤ d`, `n ∤ s` gives `n ∤ d·s`
only for prime `n`. Explicit witnesses at 8, 9, 10, 12; none at any prime.

**2.3 The resonance mechanism** — `Resonant n d := gcd(d,n) > 1`, the quantity in Thm 4.13's
cage. At prime `n` it coincides with `n ∣ d`; at composite `n` it is strictly weaker, and that
difference is where the criterion loses sets. **This is the odds/evens structure the owner
sensed**: every even order has 2 as a divisor, so essentially every set is resonant and the
criterion protects nothing; at prime orders about half are protected outright. It also reframes
order 8 — the difficulty is that **8 = 2³**, not that 8 is large.

## 4. Where order five actually stands — read this before claiming anything

**Order five is not proved.** The case split, with nothing rounded off:

A five-runner subject has at most four disparities. Partition them into commensurability classes
(`d ~ d′` when `d/d′ ∈ ℚ`).

| case | standing |
|---|---|
| at most **three** disparities | **PROVED**, unconditional, real-valued, general `n ≥ 4` — `exists_lonely_of_card_le_three` |
| four, shape `3+1` | **PROVED** today — `lonely_five_of_one_free` (§2.7), taking the singleton as `x`. A common lattice `P` for the three-class exists because every ratio inside it is rational; `x` is off it at each of them because every ratio to `x` is not |
| four, all commensurable (shape `4`) | **OPEN.** The whole-number case, where the criteria live. Residue 4 / 12 / 31 / 66 at `[1,30]` / `[1,40]` / `[1,50]` / `[1,60]`, at **flat density** — the criteria have reached a fixed point (§2.4) |
| four, shapes `2+2`, `2+1+1`, `1+1+1+1` | **OPEN.** Not proved, not claimed |

**Why the last row resists the same argument.** `lonely_opening_of_free_off_lattice` needs *all
but one* disparity on a common lattice of the chosen flank. Deleting one member of a four-set
leaves three, and in those shapes no choice of flank among the three puts the other two on a
lattice with it. Closing them needs either a **two-ratio version of `exists_clear_step`** — for
`n ≥ 5` and `1, f₁, f₂` rationally independent, an index `j` at which both are at least `1/n`
from `ℤ` — which is a genuine strengthening and is not in the tree; or an equidistribution
argument, which **firewall row six bans**. Worth putting to the author: row six's stated
justification is that the clear set at the arithmetic progression is eight isolated points of
measure zero, and that is a fact about a **rational** configuration. Whether the ban is meant to
extend to incommensurable sets is the author's ruling to make, not a session's.

Also still true:

* **P4, P6 and P7 are per-set checks**, not uniform theorems (§2.4). Only P1′, P2, P3, P5 are.
* **`FourClears` and `DoorLargestFlank`** at `n = 5` remain unproved. They are a *strengthening*
  the framework does not need (§1.4), and are on the gate's OWED/REFUTED register.
* **The five-triple statement** (`S ≥ 3/10` outside `(1,2,3)`, `(1,2,6)`, `(1,3,4)`, `(1,5,6)`,
  `(2,3,5)`) is `[EMPIRICAL]`, exact to entries ≤ 40.
* **The termination diagonal** is open at every `n ≥ 5`, unchanged. `three_diffs` needs `n ≥ 4`
  and `four_diffs` needs `n ≥ 6`, so four disparities at order five is exactly the diagonal.
