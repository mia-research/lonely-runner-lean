## 1. Mistakes, worst first

**1.1 I imported the analytical line into the framework.** I wrote `LRC/Spec/FiveInt.lean`
wiring `intLRC_five` into the cascade development. It proved nothing new (`intLRC_five` already
closes order five for integers), it mixed two Leans that are kept apart deliberately, and — the
real damage — it manufactured a "real-speeds gap" that I then reported as a finding. The
framework is **real-valued throughout** (`MANUSCRIPT_v19` §1.1; `doors_agree` carries no
rationality hypothesis). The gap was my import's, not the work's. Withdrawn to
`archive/lean_withdrawn/`. **I then reached for `intLRC_four` a second time**, in the residue
work, and the owner had to stop me again. *Do not import from the `intLRC_*` line. Ever.*

**1.2 I worked from a stale manuscript.** I used `MANUSCRIPT_v17` while v18 and v19 existed.
v19 §8.2 contains the order-5 certificate (`sup R = 31/9`, proved for ratio ≤ 26) and §1.2 the
Boundary Set Method — both directly on point. **Check `ls -t docs/n8/MANUSCRIPT_*.md` first.**

**1.3 I did covering, which the manuscript already records as a dead route.** I spent a long
stretch trying to prove "the union of blocked sets stays below `e`". v19 §8.2: *"The proof
arrives at the covering threshold by a route that uses no covering."* My blocking-capacity
figures (1.5 at `n=5`, 1.75 at `n=8`) were that coincidence re-derived.

**1.4 I imposed the largest flank on myself.** Every criterion I built targeted the *largest*
flank, though `lonely_iff_exists_flank` permits **any**. Dropping that assumption (P6) took
coverage from 80.9% to 96.1% in one step. It should have been the first thing tried, not the
last. `DoorLargestFlank` is a strengthening the framework never asked for, and it is `[REFUTED]`
at 6, 7, 8.

**1.5 Instrumentation bugs, three of them.**
* Scripts that did `sys.argv = [...]` before reading `sys.argv[1]`, so runs invoked at `S = 26`
  silently ran at `S = 18`. Two lap tables were wrong.
* An audit section reused the `n = 6` threshold while iterating 7-element sets; the rates were
  nonsense.
* A `u`-range truncated instead of reduced mod `5e`, so "93.5% coverage" was a floor presented
  as a reach.
**Every instrumented number needs a control that reproduces something already known.** The ones
that had a control (the `n = 8` refutation witness, the recorded `33/56`) caught real errors.

**1.6 I called a bootstrap circular when it was `n`-dependent.** The uniform-ceiling argument is
a legitimate bootstrap whose contraction factor is `2(n−2)/n`; it converges at `n = 3` and
diverges from `n = 4`. That is a computable failure, not a logical one. The owner corrected this.

**1.7 Random search where it cannot work.** For `DoorFirstLap` I ran ~160,000 random sets per
order and reported no counterexample — while the *known* `n = 8` witness went unfound by the same
search. A search that misses the positive it is hunting licenses no null anywhere.

**1.8 A pattern asserted before testing.** I claimed an infinite family `(3, 3m, 3m+3, 3m+6, b)`
at `n = 6` from four data points, then refuted it myself two runs later.

## 2. Contributions

**2.1 The whole `n = 8` Spec layer at general `n`** — `LRC/Spec/DoorsN.lean`, 94 declarations.
A name-by-name diff shows **all 93** `n = 8` declarations present; 105 axiom checks clean.
`doors_agree` never depended on eight: every step already passed `8` as an *argument* to a
generic lemma. What is order-specific is only the termination results.

**2.2 The prime theorem, both halves.** At prime `n`, a subject none of whose non-flank
disparities is divisible by `n` has a lonely opening at its flank —
* `n ∤ e`: `a = e·s` with `e·s ≡ 1 (mod n)`;
* `n ∣ e`: `a = k·e + 1`, `k` from a pigeonhole on `(ℤ/n)*`.
**And the reason composite orders differ is Euclid's lemma**: `n ∤ d`, `n ∤ s` gives `n ∤ d·s`
only for prime `n`. Explicit witnesses at 8, 9, 10, 12; none at any prime.

**2.3 The resonance mechanism** — `Resonant n d := gcd(d,n) > 1`, the quantity in Thm 4.13's
cage. At prime `n` it coincides with `n ∣ d`; at composite `n` it is strictly weaker, and that
difference is where the criterion loses sets. **This is the odds/evens structure the owner
sensed**: every even order has 2 as a divisor, so essentially every set is resonant and the
criterion protects nothing; at prime orders about half are protected outright. It also reframes
order 8 — the difficulty is that **8 = 2³**, not that 8 is large.

**2.4 Six criteria, all in Lean, whittling order five from 42.4% to 99.986%.**

**2.5 Negatives established, with numbers.** Covering is dead (§1.3). `move_growth` alone gives
4.106 on a word whose true `R` is 3.444. `Σ 1/u < 3/2` is false (max `113/72`). `R < n − 1` holds
at `n = 5` and **fails from `n = 6`** — the parameterised certificate gives `1482/187 > 5`,
agreeing with the manuscript's annealing maxima. So do not look for `R < n−1` at eight.

**2.6 The residue is not a different phenomenon.** Measured against the proved part: mean `|L|`
0.2095 vs 0.2119, components 13.78 vs 14.76, openings 6.18 vs 6.54. `|L| ≈ (3/5)³` and
**#openings ≈ |L|·e** (predicted 5.95, observed 6.18). Isolation happens there for the ordinary
reason; what differs is that the *tools* name a point or need a wide interval, and the residue is
where neither lands. A counting/discrepancy argument is what it wants.

## 5. Where I would go next

1. **Uniformity, not coverage.** The gain now is turning P6 into a *theorem* — a statement of
   when a good `(k, t)` exists — rather than a per-set check. The pigeonhole is weaker with a
   nonzero offset (each disparity can forbid two `k`, not one); quantifying that is the task.
2. **Port P6 to order 8.** It is order-general as written. Order 8's frontier splits by
   `gcd(d,8) ∈ {1,2,4,8}`, and P6 uses a multiple as the flank — exactly the structure 8 has most
   of. This is the most likely transfer.
3. **Do not** pursue: covering, `R < n−1` at orders above 5, `DoorLargestFlank`, or anything
   importing `intLRC_*`.
