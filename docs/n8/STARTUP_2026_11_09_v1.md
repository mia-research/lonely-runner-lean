## 1. The problem and the theorem

**The Lonely Runner Conjecture (LRC), n = 8.** Eight runners on a unit circle, distinct constant positive speeds. At any time $t$, runner $i$ sits at $t v_i \bmod 1$. LRC(n) asserts: there is a time $t$ at which some runner is at circular distance $\geq 1/n$ from every other runner ("isolated"/"lonely"). n = 8 requires distance $\geq 1/8$. LRC is open in general (known for $n \leq 7$); **this campaign proves n = 8**.

**The proof shape (the "filter assembly").** Every speed vector is reduced — through the owner's cascade framework (§2) — to a finite decision problem, then dispatched to one of finitely many certificate classes. The entire reduction exists in Lean and compiles. The main theorem, in `LonelyRunnerFresh/Fresh/FilterAssembly.lean` (line ~1318):

```lean
theorem filterAssembly (hCover : CoverData) (hCP : ClearPointOfCert)
    (hOneS : OneSidedSound) (hPair : PairwiseSound)
    (hOutside : OutsideBelow17) (hTail : TailBelow17) :
    Fresh.FilterSkeleton.AllArrangementsLandBelow17
-- composed with lonelyRunnerEight_of_allLandBelow17 → LonelyRunnerEight
```

All Lean in this campaign is **axiom-checked**: every theorem's `#print axioms` must be exactly `[propext, Classical.choice, Quot.sound]`. That is what "proved" means here — not "checked numerically", not "argued".

**The six hypotheses are the entire remaining work.** §3 gives each one's status. The single largest open piece is the **tail lemma** (§4), an analytic-combinatorial statement about speed ratios above the certified range.

## 2. The cascade, operationally

The cascade is the owner's constructive framework: it converts any arrangement into a normalized one plus an explicit displacement, and reads off where the isolation lands. You need its operational arithmetic; the authoritative definitions are `Fresh/FilterSkeleton.lean` and the module docstring of `Fresh/FilterAssembly.lean` (`IsArrangement`, `disparitySet`, `distInt`, `CascadeLandsBelow17`, `IsDmin`).

**Readings and windows.** After normalization (scale time so the best runner's minimal-disparity instant is at $R = 1$; the chosen runner is $k$, the one achieving $d_{\min}$), the other seven runners have **readings** $\rho_2 \leq \dots \leq \rho_7$ (normalized speed ratios; at order 8 these land in $[1, 17]$ — every reading's nearest integer $n_i \in [1, 17]$, giving 74,613 integer boxes after monotonicity reduction). For a reading $\rho$, the **non-lonely windows** are the open intervals

$$\frac{8j-1}{\rho} < t < \frac{8j+1}{\rho}, \qquad j = 1, 2, 3, \dots$$

($\rho t$ within distance 1 of a multiple of 8 — in these units, lonely $\iff$ $8\cdot\mathrm{dist}(\rho t/8, \mathbb{Z}) \geq 1$; the Lean object `distInt` is authoritative). **Carve of $[1,7]$**: the union of the 7 non-chosen readings' windows covers the whole interval $[1, 7]$ — the chosen runner has no lonely instant in the cascade's first window. **The theorem, in cascade language: no arrangement carves $[1,7]$** (equivalently, the cascade always lands the isolation — `CascadeLandsBelow17`: the first landing instant falls below the 17-window, i.e. the isolation is found at or before reading window 17).

**The reduction chain (all exact):**

arrangement $v$ → normalize (scale by $d_{\min}$, pick $k$) → readings $\rho \in [1,17]^7$ sorted → integer box $n \in [1,17]^7$ ($\rho_i \in (n_i - \tfrac12, n_i + \tfrac12)$, after the monotone/sorted reduction: **74,613 boxes**) → per-box certificate in one of four classes → assemble.

**The four certificate classes** (the box-level census is COMPLETE — every one of the 74,613 boxes is dispatched, OPEN = 0):

## 4. The tail mechanism (the current frontier)

Region: arrangements with a reading past 49 (normalized). State: **the mechanism is found and validated on every population tested; the formal statement is owed.** Three exact facts, all machine-verified (`experiments/n8/taillemma2_*.{py,out}`):

1. **The cycle-product law [P].** A chain type is strictly feasible only if every cycle in its used-link digraph has ratio product < 1. Links demand $\rho_q/\rho_p > (8j_q - 1)/(8j_p + 1)$; a 2-cycle with demanded product $\geq 1$ contradicts strict feasibility (= 1: tangency, equality-only, never carves; > 1: outright dead). Max over SIMPLE cycles suffices (decomposition + multiplicativity). Order edges ($\rho_p \leq \rho_{p+1}$) enter as weight-1 demands.
2. **The exact cover criterion [P; tagged [PROVISIONAL: covering]].** Windows are OPEN and $[1,7]$ compact ⟹ carve $\iff$ some strictly-overlapping chain covers $[1,7]$ on a nonempty cone. So feasibility of the demand system + no strictly-covering chain = no carve.
3. **Cone-locked gaps + iterated closure [T 27/27 branches].** For each gap in a feasible type's chain: can the type's own cone close the gap? Exact Farkas certificates say no for 150/157 gaps. The 2 types with open gaps re-route once; their continuations die by cycle product 124775/123369 > 1. **Every branch terminates.**

Validation: the backward min-relaxation reproduces the recorded tops 511/31 and 86989/3565 exactly (no LP); the E831 neighbourhood population (1,096 regenerated types) splits 1,052 dead / 17 tangency / 27 feasible-gapped, and all 27 now terminate.

**OWED for proof grade:** (a) re-route completeness — continuations were tested at LP-optimum points per open facet; all interior points (finitely many greedy continuations) must be enumerated; (b) the strip-class cap: exact middle-reading bounds from end-pins + seams, product-cap per strip class $k \geq 4$ vs $(8k-1)/7$; (c) whether any chain type reaches the lap-4 strip $\rho_7 \in (31,33)$ (E830 item 6) — box-scale enumeration needs the C certifier route, not Python (§8).
