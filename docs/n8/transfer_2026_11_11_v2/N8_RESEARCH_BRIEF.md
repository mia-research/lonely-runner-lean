# Eight Lonely Runners by finite cascade types

Research handoff. 11 November 2026. The target is **every subject in every positive, injective eight-speed arrangement**. This project has a checked cascade and a conditional proof assembly. A proof that arbitrary arrangements enter the finite classification remains open. The [fresh obligation tests](N8_OBLIGATION_TESTS.md) record exact finite replays, scope limits, and corrections to the earlier interpretations.

Use **[Lean]** for a checked theorem, **[Exact run]** for a finite computational result with its stated domain, **[Proof]** for an argument given here or in the record, and **[Open]** for a proof obligation. A tested arrangement that defeats a description identifies where that description needs refinement. Treat search limits and exhausted budgets as unresolved.

## 1. The objects and the actual cascade

Let the eight speeds be distinct positive reals $v_i$. Fix subject $k$. Each other runner gives a positive disparity $d_i=|v_i-v_k|$. Write $m=\min_i d_i$ and $\rho_i=d_i/m$. We use seven indexed readings, sorted when useful as $1=\rho_1 \le  \cdots  \le  \rho_7$, and put $R=8mt$. Equal disparities are allowed when runners are on opposite sides of the subject in speed order. Lean uses a finite set of distinct disparities where multiplicity is irrelevant. When indexing seven coordinates, repeat the reading 1 to pad.

- The subject is isolated at $t$ iff every $d_i t$ has distance at least $1/8$ from the nearest integer. In the $R$-coordinate this is $\rho_i R\bmod 8\in[1,7]$, with both endpoints clear.
- The $d_{min}$ reading excludes positive instants before $R=1$. It is clear on $W_j=[8j+1,8j+7]$, so $W_0=[1,7]$ and $W_1=[9,15]$. The interval $(7,9)$ is blocked by that reading.
- A reading $\rho$ on lap $j$ is in the way in the **open** zone $\big((8j-1)/\rho,\,(8j+1)/\rho\big)$. Start the cascade at $R=1$. If any readings are in the way, move to the largest exit of the zones containing the current point. Otherwise stay. A setter is a reading attaining that exit. The exact maximum is well defined even when setters tie.
- Each nontrivial move skips only points with a blocker. In particular, a clear witness bounds every move. Below any given finite witness, the fixed arrangement has only finitely many openings. Thus the cascade lands at the first isolation if a positive isolation exists. The finite-openings argument is for a **fixed input and a supplied witness**. [Lean, `Cascade.lean`]

A *carve* of a closed window is an open-zone cover by the readings. A clear point in either of the first two windows forces the cascade to land in their union. “Below 17” means precisely that union. The universal two-window claim is stronger than the target as stated.

### Reference isolation and displacement

On a grid $v_i=v_0+Dq_i$, $D>0$, the integer indices form a complete residue system mod 8. At $a/(8D)$ for positive odd $a$, multiplication by $a$ permutes the residues. The eight positions are equally spaced. An arithmetic progression is the grid with consecutive indices. Independent shifts by $8D c_i$ preserve the grid instants, with positive distinct speeds retained in the physical arrangement. This is the exact grid residue shift referred to in the record as a $D_{\max}$ operation. Keep it distinct from the largest disparity of a subject, $\max_i d_i$.

The proof programme is to explain where a grid isolation moves when speeds deviate. Local order-preserving perturbations can move a boundary opening, change the setter, or send the cascade into the next clear window. Grid isolation, scale invariance, and persistence in many individual families are proved. The required global statement is a route from an arbitrary input to a certified family, including all transitions. Interpolating between a grid and a sorted arrangement preserves strict speed order. The preservation of isolation along the path is still the content to prove. For an individual path, bounded witness times along an approaching sequence give a limiting witness by continuity. Strict inequalities give persistence in a neighbourhood. At a zero-margin event one must supply the next witness or a local bound. The absence of a collision supplies positivity and ordering. The bound remains to prove.

### Certified reductions worth applying first

- A subject with at most six distinct disparities is covered by the order-seven result at threshold $1/7$, hence also at $1/8$. This gives a positive witness. A claim about its location still needs a window theorem.
- If $\rho_7 \le  7$, $R=1$ is clear. The nested Lean tree has a complete route for spread at most 9 using 105 lap-assignment cones for the band from 7 to 9. The standalone kernel uses the broader pinned box route.
- The native AP branch includes any single moved runner with the other seven on a fixed progression, plus local chambers for multiple movers and families with frozen groups. The all-subject bound in `ap_nbhd_lands_12` is a speed perturbation radius $1/12$ when the AP gap is 1. The later fast variant states $1/8$. A whole-arrangement radius and a subject's reading radius are different hypotheses.
- At a fixed grid view, a residue/sign calculation settles whether the **given instant** is clear. At the class-1 instant, residues 2 through 6 are clear for deviations in $[-1/2,1/2)$; residue 1 needs nonnegative deviation, residue 7 needs nonpositive deviation, and residue 0 blocks. Later opening times depend on the deviation magnitudes and laps.

One concrete model for the kind of reduction sought is the proved two-block coalescence. Nine earlier families combine into one template, up to relabelling, common shift, scaling and reflection. It has $p$ speeds $c,c+1,\dots,c+p-1$ and $8-p$ speeds $c+A,c+A+b,\dots,c+A+(7-p)b$, with $b>0$. One of the proved regions is $2\le p\le6$ and $A-(p-1)\ge21\max(1,b)$. Every subject in these families has a witness. The actual gap for arbitrary input is to derive such a template or an equally useful transport, with hypotheses checked from the speed configuration. The more recent native opening families allow independent integer phase lifts and real bands, so they cover infinite subsets even though their family names are finite.

**A precise target for arbitrary to finite.** Prove a tail reduction $H_B$ for a usable cutoff $B$, and a bounded verification $F_B$. One strong form of $H_B$ is that every tuple with $\rho_7>B$ has a clear point in $W_0 \cup  W_1$. An equally useful form transports each such tuple to one of finitely many certified families and carries its witness back. Prove the transport on **input** conditions, including what happens at boundaries. The existing assembly fixes $B=49$ and splits the bounded region at 17. The stronger conjecture “$\rho_7 \ge  25$ implies a clear point in $W_0$” would substantially reduce the bounded work. The classical theorem permits a tail witness outside the first two windows. Such a result would require an assembly with that conclusion and would leave the two-window label claim open.

**Why bounded spread gives a finite question.** On any rectangle $1 \le  \rho_i \le   B$ with a fixed $R$-horizon $H$, the only forbidden zones meeting that horizon have $8j-1<BH$ or $8j-1 \le  BH$ if keeping boundary candidates. There are finitely many. Comparisons of their endpoints, and comparisons with fixed window endpoints, become rational linear inequalities in the readings after multiplying by positive denominators. Split by all such comparisons, retaining equality cells. The interval-cover verdict in each window is constant in a cell. This gives finite combinatorics on the bounded region before any isolation is assumed. Finiteness of this construction alone supplies no verdict and no global cutoff.

## 4. Reusable mechanism and calibrators

### Strict chain criterion [Proof]

For a fixed tuple and window $[a,b]$, list the finitely many open zones meeting the window. If they cover $[a,b]$, choose a zone containing $a$ with maximum exit. While its exit $e \le  b$, coverage at $e$ supplies a zone with entry **strictly below** $e$ and exit **strictly above** it. Choose the largest such exit. The exits increase strictly through a finite list, so the final exit exceeds $b$. Conversely a chain starting in a zone containing $a$, with these strict links and final exit greater than $b$, covers the window. Touching open zones leave the touching point clear unless another zone covers it. This criterion is the main bridge to formalize for replay. It uses the same geometry as the cascade and may be used as a finite covering tool.

A link $(p,j)\to(q,k)$ implies $\rho_q/\rho_p>(8k-1)/(8j+1)$. Multiplying around a cycle kills a strict system when the product is at least 1. A product of 1 on a cycle with only weak inequalities is allowed. Include endpoint and exit-order rows when testing feasibility. On a compact pinned box, exact rational linear feasibility with a common positive slack for strict rows is sufficient to test a candidate chain. A rational infeasibility certificate can be checked by combining rows. Do not infer exact infeasibility from solver tolerance. For joint chains use the same reading vector in both sets of inequalities.

## 5. Routes for the missing global step

**A. Use a necessary condition on a carve to eliminate the tail.** Remove the top reading. If a carve exists, each connected component of the remaining clear set in $W_0$ must fit inside one open zone of the top reading. In particular its length is strictly less than $2/\rho_7$ for closed components. Consequently, a lower bound on the longest such component in the relevant middle-reading region would cap a carve. That lower bound needs its own proof and must address zero-length components and how the middle readings enter the region. A useful compactness subgoal is to put the middle readings of a putative carve in a compact set $K$ and prove that every point of $K$ has an interior $R\in(1,7)$ where all five middle readings clear strictly. By continuity that gives a positive-length clear interval on a neighbourhood of each point. A finite subcover of $K$ gives a uniform positive minimum length. Any remaining tangency strata need their own analysis, with a $W_1$ route available if $W_0$ fails. The floating-point middle-coordinate limits in the bounded trees are observations. Some recorded near-carves are anchored at $\rho_6$, so the top reading need not provide the zone containing $R=1$. Any strip argument assuming that anchor must prove the case split.

The cycle census of 1,096 regenerated nearby types gave 1,052 infeasible by product, 17 tangent and 27 feasible with a gap. Of the last 27, 25 lock by exact certificates. Two continuations died on tested branches with product $124775/123369$. Exhausting an open facet requires its whole feasible continuation partition, beyond the tested LP optimum. The lap ladder inequality between repeat visits to the same reading gives useful pruning; the relaxed joint budget stayed feasible across the tested strips. Incorporate connectivity and actual coverage of the clear components to go further.

**B. Exploit separated scales with the actual slow-block window.** The included `TailFast` bridge handles a top reading greater than seven times every other reading, given the slow-block two-window margin `NStarAtMostOne`. `TailFastLadder` adds a fast top while decreasing the threshold from $1/k$ to $1/(k+1)$. The corpus has a checked `NStarK 5` for up to four readings. It is available as a separate closure with generated certificates. This gives the fast-top part of level 6. Full levels 6 and 7 and the comparable-top part of the tail are still separate obligations. An arbitrary-interval clear-gap estimate for blocks of five or six is false as stated. The gap register gives a short counterexample and explains what remains available on a constrained cascade window.

For the classical target, the strict fast-top case already has a shorter route without a time-window conclusion. Order seven supplies a $1/7$-clear time for the six slower disparities. If $M$ is their maximum, each stays $1/8$-clear within $1/(56M)$ of that time by the Lipschitz bound for distance to the nearest integer. The resulting interval has length $1/(28M)$. A top reading $V\ge7M$ has open forbidden intervals of length at most $1/(28M)$. Such an interval cannot contain the entire closed slow-clear interval, even at equality. This is the situation handled by the checked width lemma in ExactCellRestoration. This yields positive isolation for the fast-top case. Placing that witness in $W_0\cup W_1$ is the extra obligation for the current filter.

**C. Prove a genuine displacement transport.** Work from the positive-speed arrangement and its gap ratios or a specific grid comparison. Use the exact boundary arithmetic, the next-opening candidates on a frozen clear component, the proved return/retention and AP-block cases, and the real family hypotheses. A useful transport lemma preserves a named clear witness, or proves it transfers to a specified next window when a wall is crossed. Prove coverage of all possible transitions or reduce a well-founded quantity. The finite outcome alphabet leaves that quantity to derive. A path theorem with bounded witness times as a premise is a bridge, with the bound still to establish.

Two exact tools from the older transport work are worth keeping.

- **Whole-cell failure.** In ordinary time, let $[u,w]$ be a closed interval of simultaneous clearance for a core, with $0<u\le w$. A new reading $x>0$ blocks the whole cell iff one integer $j$ satisfies $j-1/8<xu$ and $xw<j+1/8$. Thus for positive endpoints its failure set in $x$ is a union of open intervals $\big((j-1/8)/u,\,(j+1/8)/w\big)$. Width $w-u\ge1/(4x)$ guarantees survival. To block every cell, all failure intervals must hold at the **same** $x$, and the cells come from the **same** core. The included `ExactCellRestoration` proves these generic steps. With integer core readings and integer $x$, one common period suffices. A real incoming reading requires a separate argument about successive periods.
- **Exact history and replacement.** Losing a named opening can leave the old clear window available. Losing that whole window can leave another window available, possibly earlier in the canonical cascade. A restarted cascade from a comparison time may have a different prefix. The worktree examples include captured readings returning after their own release, transfers to earlier setters with a sole blocker, and branches seen only at real interior parameters. These examples constrain proposed progress quantities. The eight-reading control $1,\dots,8$ is outside the seven-disparity target and has no $1/8$-clear instant. In the transport work it serves as a check that a proposed local progress rule uses the seven-reading restriction in an essential way.
