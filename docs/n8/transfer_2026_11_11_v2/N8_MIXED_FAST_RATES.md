# Mixed fast rates and the remaining relations

Ordinary normalized time is $t=R/8$. The target is distance at least $1/8$ to the nearest integer in one of the windows $[1/8,7/8]$ and $[9/8,15/8]$.

The plan was to derive a finite set of short integer relations for a possible obstruction, then test the first mixed harmonic profiles directly. The relation argument below forces four independent bounded relations. The exact dilation check closes 1,208 mixed five-reading profiles and 75 mixed six-reading profiles.

## Explicit families

Write each fast reading as $v_i=p_i\lambda+b_i$, where $p_i$ is a positive integer, $|b_i|\le B$ and $\lambda>0$. Use primitive slope lists, meaning their greatest common divisor is one. The all-equal case is in the common-rate proof.

- With five fast readings and slow readings $(1,a)$, every mixed slope list with $p_i\le9$ is covered if $\lambda\ge\max(40a,100B)$.
- With six fast readings and slow reading $1$, every mixed slope list with $p_i\le4$ is covered if $\lambda\ge\max(20,100B)$. The witness is in the first window.

At a base time $t$, the fast phases at its $m$-th multiple are modeled by $p_i\phi+mb_it$. Set $x_i=b_it/p_i$. For each $i$, the closed bad arcs at distance at most $q=13/100$ from an integer have centers $j/p_i-mx_i$ and radius $q/p_i$ in the free phase $\phi$. Translating all $x_i$ by the same amount rotates each phase circle. We can therefore put $x_0=0$, take every $x_i$ modulo $1/p_i$, and sort within equal slopes.

A cover of the phase circle must continue past every right endpoint. If the arc for reading $i$, indexed by $j$, ends at $R_i=(j+q)/p_i-mx_i$, some arc for a different reading $h$ must have left endpoint at most $R_i$ and right endpoint strictly greater than $R_i$. This gives two difference bounds between $x_i$ and $x_h$. A different arc from the same reading cannot continue past the endpoint since its arcs are disjoint. Laps of the continuing arc range over a finite list because $0\le x_i\le1/p_i$.

The exact replay branches over these endpoint constraints and drops a branch only if a cycle has negative total bound. To handle strict bounds, first clear denominators and then refine the grid by a factor $n+1$, where $n$ is the number of variables. Subtract one new unit for a strict inequality. Any simple cycle has at most $n$ edges, so a positive integral cycle sum stays positive. A zero sum containing a strict edge becomes negative. Thus the test handles touching arcs exactly.

For five readings, the multipliers $1,2,5$ eliminate all simultaneous covers except for $(1,1,1,3,3)$. For this profile, $(x_i)=(0,1/3,2/3,1/12,1/6)$ covers the circles at all three multipliers even at $q=1/8$. This is an obstruction to that choice of candidate times. The multipliers $1,2,3$ eliminate all covers for this profile at $q=13/100$. For six readings, $1,2,3,4,5$ eliminate all covers for all tested mixed slopes.

The common-rate proof provides a base time for the multipliers $1,2,5$ with a slow and window margin of $1/80$. For the exceptional profile use $t\in U=(11/80,23/80)$. Its first three multiples stay in the first window with margin $1/80$. The three targets for $at$ are $U$, $(91/160,149/240)$, and $1+U$. Their ratio intervals are $(11/23,23/11)$, $(91/46,149/33)$, and $(91/23,103/11)$, which overlap and cover $[1,7]$. For $a\ge7$, $aU$ has length greater than one and contains a point of an integer translate of $U$. With just the pinned reading, take $t=3/20$ as in the six-reading common-rate proof.

For the selected multiple $T=mt$, take a free phase $\phi$ outside all the closed bad arcs. Pick an integer $k$ nearest to $\lambda T-\phi$ and set $s=(k+\phi)/\lambda$. Then $|s-T|\le1/(2\lambda)$. The fast phase error comes from the drift only and is at most $B/(2\lambda)\le1/200=13/100-1/8$. The slow margins and window buffers follow from the stated lower bounds on $\lambda$. The independent rational arc sweep in the replay checks the exceptional profile and the actual lifted witnesses. The speed theorems are formalized as `Fresh.Harmonic.five_rates` and `Fresh.Harmonic.six_rates` in [the Lean kernel](lean_kernel/Fresh/HarmonicRates.lean). Each uses one `native_decide` axiom for its finite replay. The four-relation reduction below remains a paper proof. The Lean limits now extend through slope 10 (1,849 mixed five-reading profiles) and slope 5 (200 mixed six-reading profiles); see the [independent frontier replay](harmonic_frontier_check.out) for the added profiles.

## Four relations are necessary for an escaping obstruction

Here is the input reduction for arbitrary fast readings. Fix a time interval of positive length, or allow its position to vary in a compact range with its length bounded below. There exist bounds $N,D,C$ such that if all fast readings exceed $C$ and block strict clearance throughout the interval, then four linearly independent vectors $m\in\mathbb Z^k$ satisfy
$$
\|m\|_\infty\le N,\qquad |m\cdot v|\le D .
$$
The constants in this statement are existential. This section proves the reduction by a limit argument.

Suppose such bounds fail. Take a sequence with minimum fast reading tending to infinity, with no four independent relations at bounds tending to infinity. If the intervals vary, pass to a subsequence with a common interior subinterval $J$. A diagonal subsequence makes every integer frequency $m\cdot v$ either converge in $\mathbb R$ or tend to infinity in absolute value. The convergent modes form a saturated integer lattice of rank $r\le3$. Choose a matrix $A$ whose rows are a basis of that lattice, a real vector $b$ realizing the limits of those modes, and let $H$ be the kernel of $A$ in the phase torus. No coordinate is fixed on $H$. Otherwise its coordinate vector would lie in the real span of the rows of $A$, contradicting divergence of that reading.

On any subinterval of $J$, the phases converge in the sense of integrals of continuous test functions to the affine fibers $bt+H$, with uniform measure on each fiber. For a trigonometric monomial this follows by integrating its single frequency. The nonconvergent frequencies contribute zero in the limit. The convergent ones give the same integral as on the affine fibers. Uniform trigonometric approximation then gives the statement for continuous functions. A strict clear point on a fiber at an interior time gives positive integral for a continuous bump supported inside the clear cube, because the point persists in a neighborhood in the fiber and in time. Therefore a limiting obstruction requires **every** fiber $bt+H$ on $J$ to miss the open clear cube.

We now exclude those fibers for $r\le3$. The columns of $A$ have no coloop, meaning deletion of any one column preserves rank. This follows from the condition that no coordinate vector belongs to the row space.

- For $r=0$ the entire phase torus is free. For $r=1$, at least two columns are nonzero. For $r=2$ with at least four nonzero columns there are two disjoint bases. Indeed, if one parallel class has at least two columns, there must be at least two outside it, since a unique outside column would be a coloop. If every parallel class is a singleton, any two pairs suffice. Each basis maps an open coordinate box $(1/7,6/7)^r$ into a subset of the quotient torus with measure at least $(5/7)^r>1/2$. Integer nonsingular matrices push uniform torus measure forward to uniform measure. Two sets of measure greater than half have sum equal to the whole quotient torus, since one intersects every translate of the negative of the other. If $r=2$ and at most three columns are nonzero, the union bound on those coordinates suffices because each coordinate is uniform on the fiber and $3\cdot2/7<1$. Zero columns are free. Thus every target has strict clearance for $r\le2$.
- For $r=3$ with a circuit of rank at most two, first quotient out its span. A circuit is a minimal dependent set of nonzero columns. The projected columns still have no coloop, since the span after deletion was already full before projection. Solve the projected problem by the preceding case. Within the circuit span, there are at most three columns and every coordinate of their one-dimensional kernel is nonzero. Use the same union bound. These two steps compose to give strict clearance.
- In the remaining rank-three case, choose four nonzero columns. Any three are independent, so these four form a circuit. The fiber for those four coordinates has a circle component with four nonzero harmonic slopes. Each reading blocks exactly a quarter of that circle. Failure of strict clearance forces the closed arcs to tile with disjoint interiors. For any fixed cyclic order of the finitely many labeled arcs, their lengths fix all endpoints up to common rotation. Their phases modulo one are therefore fixed up to the circle action, and they give a single quotient target. There are only finitely many exceptional targets. The zero target is outside this exceptional set, since all four bad interiors overlap on the component through zero. If another nonzero column is available, its coordinate can vary inside the open clear interval, moving the target of the circuit along a nonconstant segment and avoiding the finite exceptions. If exactly four nonzero columns exist, the target is $Abt$ modulo one. It cannot remain in the finite exceptional set throughout an interval unless $Ab=0$, in which case its target is zero. This excludes persistence in rank three.

This contradiction proves the four-relation reduction. For five fast readings, a possible limiting obstruction therefore has just one rational leading direction, with bounded drifts after subtracting that direction. Its direction can be chosen as a primitive positive integer slope vector. For six fast readings, at most two rational leading directions remain. These statements follow by solving the convergent linear relations and observing that each fast coordinate diverges.

There is one further restriction in the six-reading rank-four case. A circuit of rank one or two can be solved after passing to the quotient, where the rank-three result along the same affine time curve, or the rank-two result, applies. If the smallest circuit has rank three, choose its four columns. The two remaining columns must project to two nonzero columns in the one-dimensional quotient, by the no-coloop condition. Their phases can vary along a line in the quotient fiber while staying strictly clear. Their unprojected contribution varies nontrivially within the span of the circuit, since the two columns are independent. This avoids the finite exceptional set for the four-column circuit. Consequently a six-reading rank-four obstruction can only have every four nonzero columns independent. This leaves a five-column harmonic circuit with a free coordinate, or six nonzero columns with a two-dimensional phase fiber.

The torus and quotient viewpoint is related to the published zonotope formulation of the shifted lonely runner problem. The argument here uses the time-dependent affine fiber and the stated small-rank analysis. [Malikiosis, Santos and Schymura](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.06903v2)
