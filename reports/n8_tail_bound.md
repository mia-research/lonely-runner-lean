## 0. What this delivers

The task was to prove or refute

```
   (H3u)   Tail_3(e) <= 1/12   whenever the resonances of height <= 3 span rank <= 2.
```

Neither happened. What came out instead:

1. **A different exact form of the identity** — the *box-Fourier* form (§2). `o` is the
   Haar measure of a union of 24 boxes of side `1/4` pulled back to a closed geodesic, and
   its Fourier coefficients FACTOR: `Ghat(n) = S(n mod 4) * PROD_rho A(n_rho)`. The factor
   `A` vanishes on every `n_rho = 0 (mod 4)`, so **a resonance with any coordinate
   divisible by 4 contributes nothing at all**.
2. **An exact closed form per RAY** (§3). Summing the `k`-series along a primitive
   direction `p` first gives an exact RATIONAL `Ray(p) = C(p mod 4) / PROD_{supp} p_rho`.
   The three constants `C` are bounded by `cap(2) = 3/32`, `cap(3) = 1/16`,
   `cap(4) = 5/32`, computed exactly.
3. **Theorem R1 (§4), unconditional in the height**: every rank-1 class limit lies in
   `[1/48, 1/6]`, with `1/6` attained only by the two `±1` relations. This upgrades
   `n8_o_rate.md` Theorem 5's rank-1 row from "exhaustive at height <= 5" to a theorem at
   **every** height.
4. **Theorem A (§4)**: if the generator has height `>= 4` then `|Ray(n0)| <= 1/32`, so a
   rank-1 class generated above height 3 sits within `1/32` of `3/32`.
5. **`65/256` is RESOLVED for rank 1** (§5): no rank-1 class, at any height, has that
   limit — it exceeds the proved ceiling `1/6`. Its provenance is also settled: it is a
   hard-coded `print` with no computation behind it.
6. **Theorem B (§6)**: `limsup` of `Tail_3` over a resonance class equals a DIFFERENCE OF
   TWO CLASS LIMITS, `Delta(M') = o_inf(M') - o_inf(M_3(M'))`. So (H3u) implies
   `Delta(M') <= 1/12` on every realisable saturated `M'` of rank `<= 2` — a statement
   about pairs of lattices with no speeds in it, and the natural place to look for a
   counterexample.
7. **Theorem C (§7): the termwise route is dead, quantitatively.** `n8_o_rate.md` §9a
   argued this from a crude pair bound. Here it is pinned to a tuple that actually lies in
   the (H3u) arm: at `e = (17,3,21,7)`, which has `rank L_3 = 2` and `Tail_3 = 8/153`,
   the ray-level absolute sum truncated at ray height 100 is already `0.1784`, i.e.
   **2.14x the whole `1/12` budget**, with the `|supp| = 4` block alone at `0.1235`.
   Any proof of (H3u) must use cancellation inside that block.
8. **The class-level search (§8) found no violation.** Over all 10 120 realisable
   rank-2 classes generated at height `<= 4` whose height-`<=3` part has rank `<= 1`, the
   largest gap is `Delta = 47/1056 = 0.0445`, i.e. `53.4%` of the `1/12` budget; over the
   3580 realisable classes through the 18 lowest-limit rank-1 classes with a second
   generator of height `<= 6` — the arm with the tightest budget — it is
   `337/11616 = 0.0290`, `34.8%`. Zero violations in either arm.
9. **Coverage corrections to `n8_o_rate.md` Theorem 5** (§9). Its realisability filter is
   an `alpha`-parameter box, the same failure mode its own §11(a) records: the true counts
   are **108** rank-1 and **1964** rank-2 realisable classes at height `<= 3`, not 107 and
   1795, with an explicit witness for every one. Both of its MAXIMA (`1/6`, `1/4`) survive
   the recount. Separately, its list of the height-`<=3` rank-1 limits omits two values,
   `7/72` and `79/864`.

`(H3u)` is left **OPEN**, with the obstruction named in §7 and the reduction of §6 as the
place to attack it. The one structural pointer the search produced: the budget is consumed
by SHALLOW class members overshooting their own class limit, not by deep members sitting
at a large `Delta` (§8, last paragraph).

## 1. Notation

`e = (e_1,e_3,e_5,e_7)`, `e_rho > 0`, `e_rho = rho (mod 8)`, `gcd = 1`.
`theta` runs over `[0,1)`; `a_rho(theta) = floor(4 frac(e_rho theta))` in `{0,1,2,3}`;
`mu = (0,1,2,3)`; `nu_rho = a_rho XOR mu_rho`; `o = |{theta : nu all distinct}|`.

`L(e) = { n in Z^4 : n.e = 0 }` (rank 3, saturated). `L_R(e)` is the subgroup generated by
the `n in L(e)` with `||n||_inf <= R`; `M := sat L_3(e)`; `o_inf(M')` is the Haar average
of the good indicator over the subtorus killed by `M'`; `Tail_3(e) = o(e) - o_inf(M)`.

A vector `n` is **admissible** if `n != 0`, `gcd(n) = 1`, `SUM n_rho rho = 0 (mod 8)`,
`|supp n| >= 2`, and `n` has both a positive and a negative coordinate. Every resonance of
a positive unit tuple is a multiple of an admissible vector.

## 2. The box-Fourier form  `[PROVED]`

Let `G` be the indicator on `T^4` of the union of the 24 boxes
`PROD_rho [c_rho/4, (c_rho+1)/4)` over the good `c` (those with `c_rho XOR mu_rho` all
distinct). Then `o(e) = INT_0^1 G(e theta) d theta`, `|G| = 24/256 = 3/32`.

> **Proposition 2.1 (factorisation).** For `n in Z^4`,
> ```
>     Ghat(n) = S(n mod 4) * PROD_rho A(n_rho),
>     A(m) = (1/4) INT_0^1 e(-mu/4) du = 1/4  (m = 0),  (1 - e(-m/4))/(2 pi i m)  else,
>     S(j) = SUM over the 24 good c of (-i)^{SUM_rho j_rho c_rho}   in Z[i].
> ```
> Consequently `|A(m)| = |sin(pi m/4)|/(pi|m|)`, so
> ```
>     A(m) = 0  for every  m = 0 (mod 4),  m != 0.
> ```

*Proof.* `1[floor(4v) = c]` has Fourier coefficients `e(-nc/4) A(n)`; multiply over `rho`
and sum over the 24 good `c`. `|1 - e(-m/4)| = 2|sin(pi m/4)|`. ∎

`S` is pure group data. Computed exactly (`tb_fourier_v1.py`): `S(0) = 24`; `S = 0` on
every `j` with a single nonzero coordinate; `max |S| = 8` on `|supp j| = 2` and `3`, and
`24` on `|supp j| = 4`; `SUM_j |S(j)|^2 = 6144 = 256 * 24` (Parseval, exact).

> **Proposition 2.2 (the identity).** `SUM_{n in L(e)} |Ghat(n)| < infinity`, and
> ```
>     o(e) = SUM_{n in L(e)} Ghat(n).
> ```

*Proof.* Absolute convergence: `|Ghat(n)| <= 24 (1/4)^{4-|S|} PROD_{rho in supp} 1/(pi
|n_rho|)`, and for each support `S` the sum `T_S = SUM_{n in L, supp n = S} PROD_S
1/|n_rho|` is finite. `T_S` is the increasing limit of
`INT_0^1 PROD_{rho in S} phi_N(e_rho theta) d theta` with
`phi_N(t) = SUM_{0<|n|<=N} e(nt)/|n|` — all terms of that integral are positive — and by
Hoelder `|INT PROD phi_N| <= ||phi_N||_{|S|}^{|S|}`, which is bounded because
`phi_N -> phi = -2 log|2 sin pi t|` in `L^p` for `1 < p < infinity` (M. Riesz) and
`phi in L^4`, `INT_0^1 (2 log|2 sin pi t|)^4 dt = 19 pi^4 / 15`.

The identity: let `G_N` be the 4-fold product Fejer mean. Because `G` is a finite sum of
products of one-dimensional interval indicators, `G_N` is the corresponding sum of products
of one-dimensional Fejer means, so `0 <= G_N <= 24` and `G_N(v) -> G(v)` at every `v` with
no coordinate in `(1/4)Z`. The geodesic `theta -> e theta` meets that null set in finitely
many `theta`, so `G_N(e theta) -> G(e theta)` for a.e. `theta`; bounded convergence gives
`INT G_N(e theta) d theta -> o(e)`. On the other side
`INT G_N(e theta) d theta = SUM_{n in L} w_N(n) Ghat(n)` with `0 <= w_N <= 1`,
`w_N(n) -> 1`, and dominated convergence for series applies by the first paragraph. ∎

This supplies the interchange that `n8_o_rate.md` §9 fact 1 states "formally, not proved
here".

**Verification.** `tb_fourier_v1.py` checks `Ghat` against a direct numerical integral
(`max error 2.7e-18` over 12 random `n`) and checks the truncated identity against the
exact `o` on six tuples: the partial sums approach `o` from below with error `~3e-3` at
truncation height 120, consistent with the `1/N` rate of a conditionally-arranged sum.

## 3. The exact per-ray closed form  `[PROVED]`

Every nonzero `n` is `k p` for a unique primitive `p` up to sign. Summing the `k`-series
first is exactly summable.

> **Theorem 3.1.** Let `p` be primitive with support `T`, `s = |T|`, and set
> `W_p(r) = S(r p) PROD_{rho in T} (1 - (-i)^{r p_rho})`. Then
> ```
>   Ray(p) := SUM_{k != 0} Ghat(k p)
>           = (1/4)^{4-s} / ( (2 pi i)^s PROD_{rho in T} p_rho ) * SUM_{r=1,2,3} W_p(r) Z_s(r)
> ```
> with `Z_s(r) = SUM_{k = r (4), k != 0} k^{-s}`, namely
> `Z_2(1) = Z_2(3) = pi^2/8`, `Z_2(2) = pi^2/16`;
> `Z_3(1) = -Z_3(3) = pi^3/32`, `Z_3(2) = 0`;
> `Z_4(1) = Z_4(3) = pi^4/96`, `Z_4(2) = pi^4/768`.
> Every power of `pi` cancels, so
> ```
>   s = 2:  Ray(p) = -[ W(1)/8 + W(2)/16 + W(3)/8 ] / (64 PROD p)
>   s = 3:  Ray(p) =  i [ W(1) - W(3) ] / (1024 PROD p)
>   s = 4:  Ray(p) =  [ (W(1)+W(3))/96 + W(2)/768 ] / (16 PROD p)
> ```
> and **`Ray(p)` is a rational number**. If any `p_rho` (`rho in T`) is `0 (mod 4)` then
> `Ray(p) = 0` exactly.

*Proof.* `A(k p_rho) = (1 - (-i)^{k p_rho})/(2 pi i k p_rho)` and both `S(kp)` and
`(-i)^{k p_rho}` depend on `k` only mod 4; `W_p(0) = 0` because `1 - (-i)^0 = 0`. The
`Z_s(r)` are the standard values (`Z_2`: `SUM_{odd} m^-2 = pi^2/8`; `Z_3(1)` is the
Dirichlet beta value `beta(3) = pi^3/32`; `Z_4`: `SUM_{odd} m^-4 = pi^4/96`). Rationality
is checked in the code by asserting that the imaginary part is exactly `0`. ∎

> **Lemma 3.2 (the caps).** `|Ray(p)| * PROD_{rho in T} |p_rho|` depends only on
> `(p mod 4, s)`. Over the residue patterns a primitive resonance of an all-odd speed tuple
> can have — no support coordinate `0 (mod 4)`, an EVEN number of odd coordinates (because
> `SUM n_rho e_rho = 0` with all `e_rho` odd forces `SUM n_rho` even), and not all support
> coordinates `2 (mod 4)` (primitivity) — the exact maxima are
> ```
>     cap(2) = 3/32,   cap(3) = 1/16,   cap(4) = 5/32,     CAP := 5/32.
> ```

`cap(2) = 3/32` reproduces Corollary 3.1 of `n8_o_rate.md` (`(24/256)/(ab) = 3/(32ab)`) by
a completely different route, which is a useful cross-check of both.

**Verification.** `tb_ray_v1.py` sums `Ray(p)` over primitive rays of `L(e)` up to height
`N` and compares with the exact `o(e) - 3/32`: on six tuples the partial sums converge to
the exact value (errors `4e-4` to `3e-3` at `N = 120`).

## 4. Two unconditional theorems  `[PROVED]`

`PI(p) := PROD_{rho in supp p} |p_rho|`, so `|Ray(p)| <= CAP / PI(p) = (5/32)/PI(p)`.

> **Theorem R1.** For every admissible primitive `n0`, at every height,
> ```
>     |o_inf(<n0>) - 3/32| = |Ray(n0)| <= 7/96,
> ```
> so **every rank-1 class limit lies in `[1/48, 1/6]`**. The value `7/96` is attained
> exactly at the four sign representatives of the two `±1` relations `(1,1,-1,-1)` and
> `(1,-1,-1,1)`, where `o_inf = 3/32 + 7/96 = 1/6`.

*Proof.* If `ht(n0) >= 3` then `PI(n0) >= 3` and `|Ray| <= (5/32)/3 = 5/96 < 7/96`. The
vectors of height `<= 2` are a finite list; exhaustively, `max |Ray| = 7/96`. (The code
runs the finite check to height 9 and covers `ht > 9` by `CAP/10 = 1/64`, which is a wider
margin than needed.) ∎

> **Theorem A (the rank-1 increment).** If `ht(n0) >= 4` then `|Ray(n0)| <= 1/32`,
> attained at `n0 = (-5,1,-1,1)`.

*Proof.* A support coordinate divisible by 4 makes `Ray` vanish (Prop 2.1), so either
`Ray(n0) = 0` or `max |n0_rho|` is not divisible by 4 and hence `>= 5`; then `PI >= 5` and
`|Ray| <= (5/32)/5 = 1/32`. Exhaustively over `4 <= ht <= 9` the maximum is exactly `1/32`;
`ht > 9` gives `<= 1/64`. ∎

The by-height table of `max |Ray|` (exact, `tb_caps_v1.py`):

| `ht` | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 | 6 | 7 | 8 | 9 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `max abs Ray` | `7/96` | `1/32` | `5/96` | `0` | `1/32` | `1/96` | `3/224` | `0` | `7/864` |

Heights 4 and 8 are exactly `0`: every admissible vector of height 4 (resp. 8) has a
coordinate `±4` (resp. `±8`), which kills the ray.

**Consequence for the report's Theorem 5.** `n8_o_rate.md` grades "rank-1 maximum `1/6` at
height `<= 5` (612 classes)" as PROVED-at-that-height and leaves the general question open.
Theorem R1 closes the rank-1 half of it at every height.

## 5. `65/256`  `[RESOLVED for rank 1; provenance settled]`

`n8_o_rate.md` §11(b) leaves this `[UNRESOLVED]`:

> `| the next height class | `65/256 ≈ 0.25391` |`

Two findings.

**(a) It is not a rank-1 class limit, at any height.** `65/256 - 3/32 = 41/256`, and
`41/256 > 40/256 = 5/32 = CAP >= |Ray(n0)|`; by Theorem R1 the true ceiling is the smaller
`1/6 = 0.1666...`. So the constant cannot arise as a rank-1 limit. `[PROVED]`

**(b) Provenance.** The only occurrence of the number anywhere in the tree outside the two
documents is `experiments/n8/hp_resonance_limit_v1.py` line 45:

```
print("65/256 =", float(F(65,256)))
```

which is a **hard-coded constant printed for comparison** — nothing computes it. That file's
`limit()` routine, over every coefficient triple in `(-3..3)^3` and five different `d`-laws
(including the `1/6, 2/3, 1/6` law it actually uses), produces only the values
`0, 1/24, 1/16, 5/64, 1/12, 3/32, 7/64, 5/48, 1/8, 9/64, 1/6, 3/16, 9/32, 1/4, 3/8` —
`65/256` is not among them (`tb_diag`/inline check, exact `Fraction` arithmetic). The commit
that introduced both the script and the table row (`6eff20f`) asserts the value in its
message as well, with the phrase "confirmed numerically to 9e-6" — but the only `9e-6` in
the evidence is the convergence of the `1/6` family (`9.05e-06` at
`e = (73,1283,1933,3143)`), which is a different row of the same table.

**Verdict.** `65/256` is a hand-entered constant with no derivation in any surviving
artifact, it is impossible as a rank-1 limit, and it exceeds `1/4`, the observed maximum
over rank-2 classes. It should be treated as **withdrawn**, not merely unresolved. What is
not proved here is that no rank-2 class attains it; that is a strictly weaker residue of
the question and it coincides with the already-open "is `1/4` the supremum over rank-`<=2`
classes".

## 6. What (H3u) asks, restated without speeds  `[PROVED]`

> **Theorem B.** Let `M'` be a saturated sublattice of `Z^4` of rank `<= 2` that is
> realisable (some positive primitive unit tuple lies in its kernel). Put
> ```
>     M_3(M') = saturation of the subgroup generated by the height-<=3 vectors of M',
>     Delta(M') = o_inf(M') - o_inf(M_3(M')).
> ```
> Then over the members `e` of the class,
> ```
>     limsup Tail_3(e) = Delta(M')   as max e_rho -> infinity within the class.
> ```
> Consequently a realisable `M'` with `Delta(M') > 1/12` REFUTES (H3u), and
> `Delta(M') <= 1/12` for every realisable `M'` is a NECESSARY condition for it.

*Proof.* Two halves.

*(i) `sat L_3(e) = M_3(M')` for all deep members.* If `n in L(e)` has height `<= 3` and
`n not in M'`, then `M' + Zn` has rank 3 and lies in `L(e)`, so by the determinant
argument of `n8_o_rate.md` Theorem 4, `max e_rho <= 3 sqrt3 H^3` with `H` the largest
height among a generating set of `M'` together with `n`. Beyond that bound every
height-`<=3` resonance of `e` lies in `M'`.

*(ii) `o(e) -> o_inf(M')` within the class.* With `B` a basis of the saturated kernel of
`M'` and `e = B alpha`, the closure of the orbit is the subtorus `T_{M'}`, and the
resonances of the geodesic inside it are the `kappa in Z^{d}` (`d = 4 - rank M'`) with
`kappa . alpha = 0`. For `rank M' = 2` this is the line `Z m_0`, `m_0 = alpha^perp`; the
good region in `T^2` is a union of cells of the arrangement of the lines
`B_rho . y in (1/4)Z`, whose edge normals are the `B_rho`, and
`det(B_rho, m_0) = B_rho . alpha = e_rho != 0`, so no boundary edge is parallel to the
geodesic. Two integrations by parts over the cell boundaries then give
```
    |Ghat_{M'}(k m_0)|  <=  (1 / (pi^2 k^2 |alpha|)) * SUM_rho E_rho |B_rho| / e_rho ,
```
`E_rho` being the number of boundary edges of the good region lying on family-`rho` lines
(a quantity of the class alone, independent of `alpha`), so
`SUM_{k != 0} |Ghat_{M'}(k m_0)| = O(1/|alpha|) -> 0`. Only the vanishing is used here;
the constant is revisited in §10. For `rank M' <= 1` the
same conclusion follows from Proposition 2.2 directly: the rays of `L(e) \ M'` all have
height `> R` with `R -> infinity` by (i)'s determinant argument, and the ray sum is
absolutely convergent. ∎

**Control (the plant must fire).** §8 checks Theorem B on the maximiser of its arm 3: the
exact `Tail_3` of deep members of that class does descend towards its `Delta`, while a
shallow member of the same class sits well above it. And realisability is not a formality:
a first pass produced the class `((1,2,-1,-2),(0,3,-1,0))` with the large gap
`Delta = 5/72 = 0.0694`, but its kernel forces `e_5 = 3 e_3`, which is incompatible with
`e_5 = 5` and `e_3 = 3 (mod 8)` — the class has NO witness and the gap is vacuous. §8
decides realisability exactly for that reason.

## 7. Why the termwise bound cannot work  `[PROVED at the stated tuple]`

The sharpest bound of triangle-inequality type available in this framework is

```
    |Tail_3(e)|  <=  ABS(e) := SUM over primitive rays p in L(e) \ M of |Ray(p)| ,
```

which is strictly sharper than putting absolute values on individual frequencies, because
the `k`-series along each ray has already been summed in closed form (Theorem 3.1) — and
that sum does cancel: `Z_3(2) = 0` and `Z_3(1) = -Z_3(3)`.

> **Theorem C.** It is still not enough. At `e = (17,3,21,7)`, which lies in the (H3u) arm
> (`rank L_3(e) = 2`, `sat L_3(e)` of limit `2/9`, `o = 14/51`, `Tail_3 = 8/153 = 0.0522876`), the
> partial sum of `ABS` over rays of height `<= 100` is already
> ```
>     0.178387  =  2.14 x the whole 1/12 budget,
> ```
> and the `|supp| = 4` block alone contributes `0.123472 = 1.48 x` the budget.
> Since `ABS` is a sum of positive terms, the partial sum is a lower bound for `ABS`.

Four more tuples, same truncation (ray height 100); the last is a deep tuple where
the bound is comfortable, included so the table is not selected on its conclusion:

| `e` | `rank L_3` | `o_inf` | `Tail_3` | `ABS >=` | `s=2` | `s=3` | `s=4` | `ABS/budget` |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `(17,3,21,7)` | 2 | `2/9` | 0.052288 | 0.178387 | 0.016369 | 0.038554 | 0.123472 | 2.14 |
| `(25,11,77,7)` | 1 | `1/8` | 0.072403 | 0.131217 | 0.017305 | 0.013530 | 0.100381 | 1.57 |
| `(65,51,5,7)` | 1 | `1/8` | 0.036689 | 0.111986 | 0.003967 | 0.024742 | 0.083277 | 1.34 |
| `(49,3,13,23)` | 2 | `3/16` | 0.042245 | 0.099495 | 0.003306 | 0.019326 | 0.076863 | 1.19 |
| `(241,163,205,199)` | 1 | `1/6` | 0.002711 | 0.007675 | 0.000000 | 0.003920 | 0.003755 | 0.09 |

**This is the named obstruction.** `n8_o_rate.md` §9a reached the same conclusion from a
crude pair bound at `R = 3` (`9/(16*4) = 0.1406`) and a comparison of `ABS` against the
deviation from `3/32` rather than against `Tail_3`. The statement above is the same
obstruction measured against the right quantity, on tuples that are actually in the arm
(H3u) quantifies over, with the blocks separated: **the `|supp| = 4` block alone exceeds
the budget, so (H3u) cannot be proved without cancellation between full-support rays.**

## 8. The class-level search  `[EMPIRICAL — exhaustive over the stated height boxes]`

By Theorem B a violation of (H3u) would show up as a realisable saturated `M'` of rank
`<= 2` with `Delta(M') > 1/12`. Three arms were run; **none found one**.

**Populations (requirement (iii)).** Every arm below is a **parameter box over generator
HEIGHT** (`||n||_inf <= HMAX`), never a speed box. Realisability is decided exactly — the
open-cone plus mod-8 test of `tb_hunt_v3.py`, cross-validated against
`rt_limit_v2.witnesses(PMAX=30)` on all 3382 height-`<=3` planes with **0** disagreements —
so no `alpha` box enters any verdict. Where witnesses are printed their true maximum speed
is shown. Class limits are exact `Fraction`s from `tb_limit_v1.rank2_limit`, a
depth-first-clipping rewrite that agrees with `rt_limit_v2.rank2_limit` on all 3382
height-`<=3` lattices and on 60 random height-`<=6` lattices (**0** mismatches, 19x faster).

**Arm 1 — rank-1 `M'`, height `<= 4` (`tb_hunt_v3.py 4`).** 272 distinct rank-1 classes.
`max o_inf = 1/6`, `min o_inf = 1/24`. Restricted to generators of height `>= 4`, where
Theorem A applies: `max o_inf = 3/32` and hence `max Delta = 0`. Consistent with Theorem A
(`|Delta| <= 1/32`) and much stronger than it in this box.

**Arm 2 — rank-2 `M'`, height `<= 4`, both sub-arms (`tb_hunt_v3.py 4 2000000`).**
21 474 distinct 2-planes spanned by admissible vectors of height `<= 4`:
1964 have a rank-2 height-`<=3` part (`Delta = 0` identically), 9390 are not realisable,

* **`MAX Delta = 47/1056 = 0.0445076`**, i.e. `53.4%` of the `1/12` budget, attained on
  12 classes with `o_inf = 157/1056` over a height-`<=3` part of limit `5/48`; the first is
  `((1,11,-2,0),(0,16,-3,1))`.
* Count with `Delta > 1/12`: **0**.
* Largest `Delta` in the sub-arm with **no** height-`<=3` vector at all (`M_3 = 0`,
