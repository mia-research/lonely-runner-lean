## Finding 1 — CLOSED: Theorem 8.2c non-unit gap — RESOLVED ANALYTICALLY
**Resolution file:** `lrc_8_2c_nonunit_closure_2026-03-12.md`
**Computational verification:** 158 (n,d) pairs, composite n ∈ {4..30}, zero failures.

### Original finding (for record)


**Location:** §8.2c, lines 219–238; used in §10.2 Sub-case B2 (line 512) and §8.3 CRT-G Tier 1 (line 356).

**What the proof does:**
Theorem 8.2c finds a specific z=0 event (fibre b_m = mL/e_u) and shows:
- No **unit** runner satisfies Cond 1 (because e_u ∤ (m-1) for 1 ≤ m-1 < e_u)
- The unique **unit** Cond 2 runner is d (the dropped non-unit)
- Therefore the departing residue r_dep is "uncovered by all retained runners."

**The gap:**
Cond 1 / Cond 2 (Lemma 8.2a) describes coverage by **unit** runners only. Unit bad sets have exactly 2 elements; non-unit bad sets are cosets of H_g = (n/g)Z_n (g elements each). The Lemma 8.2a proof derivation only applies when gcd(e',n)=1.

For a retained non-unit runner e' ∈ E_fix with g = gcd(e',n) > 1:
- bad(e', t, n) ≠ ∅ iff g | t or g | (t+1) where t = ⌊e'm/e_u⌋
- If non-empty, bad(e', t, n) is a coset of H_g and may contain r_dep

The proof of Theorem 8.2c says nothing about this case. The Cond 2 analysis proves uniqueness of the unit Cond 2 solution = d, but non-unit runners cover via a completely different mechanism.

**Concrete test (n=15, d=10):**
Setting n=15, d=10, u=2, e_u=13, computes m=6, r_dep=10.
Checking retained non-unit runners e' ∈ {3,5,6,9}:
- e'=3: t=⌊18/13⌋=1. gcd(3,15)=3: 3∤1, 3∤2 → bad=∅. r_dep not covered. ✓
- e'=5: t=⌊30/13⌋=2. gcd(5,15)=5: 5∤2, 5∤3 → bad=∅. r_dep not covered. ✓
- e'=6: t=⌊36/13⌋=2. gcd(6,15)=3: 3∤2 → Sol(-2,15)=∅; 3|3 → Sol(-3,15)={r: 6r≡-3(15)}={2,7,12}. r_dep=10 ∉ {2,7,12}. ✓
- e'=9: t=⌊54/13⌋=4. 3∤4, 3∤5 → bad=∅. r_dep not covered. ✓

The gap didn't cause failure here, but **the proof never checks this**. It could fail for some (n, d, e') triple where floor states align with g | t AND r_dep lands in the non-unit coset.

**Why this matters:**
Both Sub-case B2 Tier 1 and CRT-G Tier 1 (Case G, multi-prime composite n) cite Theorem 8.2c for their Tier 1 conclusion. If retained non-unit runners can cover r_dep at the targeted event, those Tier 1 claims are not proven.

**What needs to happen (NOT mere branch-splitting):**
The right question is: WHY can't a non-unit retained runner cover r_dep at the specific fibre b_m? Is it:
- (A) Their floor state at b_m never satisfies g | t (structural reason related to m)?
- (B) Even when g | t, r_dep doesn't lie in their bad-set coset (structural reason related to r_dep)?
- (C) Some combination?

The answer should be a SINGLE additional lemma about the floor-state geometry at the targeted event — not a new branch per runner type. The mechanism is either "non-unit bad sets are empty at this fibre" (floor-state reason) or "r_dep is structurally excluded from all non-unit cosets" (residue-type reason). If (B), it would likely follow from a property of r_dep's relationship to d's prime structure.

**Priority:** HIGH — this blocks Tier 1 for all composite n with ≥2 prime factors, Case G.

## Finding 2 — HONEST DISCLOSURE NEEDED: Tier 1 for small cases is computational

**Location:** §12 line 569: "Each branch achieves x₀ ≥ 1 analytically throughout."

**What's actually true:**
The Tier 1 claim includes escape hatches:
- Prime n ≤ 30, small M: handled by enumeration (Appendix A.4)
- Composite n ≤ 12, small L: handled by enumeration (Appendix A.5)

**Priority:** LOW for correctness (enumerations are valid), HIGH for referee presentation.

## Finding 3 — PP-T2 INTERNAL ERROR: Step 4 contradicts Step 2 for k=3, p=3

**Location:** §8.3 lines 406–417 (Theorem PP-T2 proof, Steps 2 and 4).

**The error:**
- Step 2 shows D' ≤ 0.91 for k=3, p=3 (n=27).
- Step 4 asserts "D' < 1/2" to bound B_safe ≥ φ(n)(n-2)/2.
- 0.91 < 1 does NOT imply D' < 1/2. The Step 4 bound fails for n=27.

**Tier 1 impact:** None. PP-T2 is a Tier 2 theorem.
**Tier 2 impact:** PP-T2 is unproven for n=27 (k=3, p=3). The other open items for PP-T2 (PL-KINDEP) are already disclosed, but this is an ADDITIONAL unpublicized bug.

**Priority:** MEDIUM — affects Tier 2 but the right fix is likely refining the D' bound, not restructuring.

## Finding 4 — PL-KINDEP proof is wrong as written (confirming other agent)

**Location:** §8.3 lines 388–390.

**The claim:** "For distinct e_i, e_j: their window systems have coprime periods."

**Why this is wrong:** L = lcm(E_fix). L/e_i and L/e_j are NOT coprime in general. Example: n=9, e_i=3, e_j=6 → L/e_i = L/3, L/e_j = L/6, gcd = L/6 ≠ 1.

This confirms the other agent's finding. PL-KINDEP's proof is built on the wrong structural unit (individual runners rather than mod-p groups). Already disclosed as an open item in §12.

**Priority:** Already tracked. Confirmed incorrect.

## Recommended Next Action

**For Finding 1 — the right investigation, not a new branch:**

Do NOT create a sub-case for "non-unit runners in Case G." Instead, answer this question analytically:

> At fibre b_m = mL/e_u where m is the Theorem 8.2c witness index, is it true that for every prime g | n and every retained non-unit runner e' with gcd(e',n) = g, we have g ∤ ⌊e'm/e_u⌋ AND g ∤ (⌊e'm/e_u⌋ + 1)?

If YES: add a lemma "Non-Unit Transparency at Targeted Event" with an analytic proof. One safe fibre is enough for Tier 1 — if the floor states of non-unit runners are not dangerous at b_m, Tier 1 is clean.

If NO (there exist e' where g | floor state): then also check whether r_dep lies in the corresponding non-unit coset. If r_dep is excluded from that coset by its own prime structure, the gap is still closed but by a different mechanism.

This is the same methodology as the B2 → Thm 8.2c transition: understand WHY a specific thing cannot happen rather than branching around it.
