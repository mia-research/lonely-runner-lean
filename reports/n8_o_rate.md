## 0. What this delivers

The task asked for an effective bound `|o − o_inf| <= C/H(e)` reducing (H'') to a finite
check. What came out has a different shape, and §9 records why the requested shape cannot
hold for the whole deviation:

1. **An exact identity replaces the equidistribution heuristic.** `o` is *exactly* a
   finite character sum over resonances (Thm 1). The value `3/32` is not a limit under an
   assumption — it is the `w = 0` term of an identity, and every deviation from it is a
   resonance sum. §3 of `O_RESONANCE_v1` graded the limits `[DERIVED]` under
   equidistribution; the identity and the class limits below are `[PROVED]`.
2. **The pair part of the deviation obeys exactly the requested bound**, with an explicit
   constant and a sharp inequality (Thm 3): `9/(16 R)`.
3. **A rate of the form `C/H(e)` cannot exist for the full deviation**, and the reason is
   structural, not technical (§9). What replaces it is a *class-relative* bound, and the
   class is the saturated lattice generated by the low-height resonances.
4. **The class limits are computable exactly and are all `<= 1/4`** over every class
   generated at height `<= 3` (Thm 5): 107 rank-1 classes with maximum `1/6`, 1795 rank-2
   classes with maximum `1/4`.
5. **A reduction of (H'') to one inequality plus a finite check that has been executed**
   (Thm 6, §7). At height `R = 3` the finite arm is 665 tuples, all of speed `<= 95`, and
   all are inside an exhaustive census to speed `200` that finds nothing above `1/3`
   outside the two exceptions. What is left is the single uniform inequality
   ```
       (H3u)   Tail_3(e) <= 1/12   whenever the resonances of height <= 3 span rank <= 2,
   ```
   measured to hold with `13%` of margin over the whole box `max e_rho <= 200`.

No counterexample to (H'') was found. One coverage defect and one unreproduced constant in
the predecessor document are recorded, with quotations, in §11; a third claim I initially
took for a defect turned out to be correct and is withdrawn there.

## 1. The exact character expansion  `[PROVED]`

Let `V = {0,1,2,3}` be the Klein group under bitwise XOR, with characters
`psi_w(v) = (-1)^popcount(w AND v)`. Put `f_w(t) = psi_w(floor(4 frac(t)))`, a `+-1` step
function on the circle. For `mu = (0,1,2,3)` indexed by `rho = 1,3,5,7`:

> **Theorem 1.**
> ```
>     o(e) = (1/256) * SUM_{w in V^4} K(w) eps(w) I(w),
> ```
> where `K(w) = SUM over the 24 bijections v of PROD_rho psi_{w_rho}(v_rho)`,
> `eps(w) = PROD_rho psi_{w_rho}(mu_rho)`, and
> `I(w) = INT_0^1 PROD_rho f_{w_rho}(e_rho theta) d theta`.

*Proof.* Expand each `1[nu_rho = v_rho]` by Klein-group Fourier inversion, sum over the
24 bijections, and use `psi_w(a XOR mu) = psi_w(a) psi_w(mu)` with Fubini. ∎

`K` and `eps` are pure group data, independent of `e`. Computed exactly
(`rt_char_v1.py`):

| `|supp w|` | vectors | `K != 0` | `SUM |K|` | `max |K|` |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | 1 | 1 | 24 | 24 |
| 1 | 12 | **0** | 0 | 0 |
| 2 | 54 | 18 | 144 | 8 |
| 3 | 108 | 24 | 192 | 8 |
| 4 | 81 | 21 | 216 | 24 |

So `K(0) = 24` and the `w = 0` term is `24/256 = 3/32` **exactly**, and `K` vanishes on
every `w` with a single nonzero coordinate. Hence

> **Corollary 1.1.** `o(e) − 3/32` is a sum of `63` terms, each a resonance sum of a pair,
> triple or quadruple of the speeds. There is no first-order term.

**Verification.** `rt_char_v1.py` recomputes `o` from the identity in exact `Fraction`
arithmetic and compares with the direct cell count, on ten tuples spanning the extremal
and generic cases — `(1,3,5,7)`, `(1,11,5,7)`, `(17,3,5,7)`, `(1,3,13,15)`, `(9,27,5,23)`,
`(9,3,5,7)`, `(25,11,13,39)`, `(41,43,13,15)`, `(33,27,13,7)`, `(9,27,13,31)`. **Ten of
ten agree exactly.** Boundary audits `(cells, 0, 0)` on all of them.

## 2. A vanishing rule  `[PROVED]`

Expanding `f_w` in Fourier series: `f_0 = 1`; `f_1(t) = r(2t)` is supported on frequencies
`n = 2 (mod 4)` with `|f_1^(n)| = 4/(pi |n|)`; `f_2(t) = r(t)` and `f_3(t) = r(t + 1/4)`
are supported on odd `n` with `|f^(n)| = 2/(pi |n|)`, where `r` is the `+-1` square wave.
All three have mean zero, so `I(w)` is a sum over resonances `n . e = 0` with `n_rho != 0`
exactly on `supp(w)`.

> **Theorem 2.** `I(w) = 0` unless `#{rho : w_rho in {2,3}}` is even.

*Proof.* Those `rho` are exactly the ones with `n_rho` odd; the rest have `n_rho` even.
`SUM n_rho e_rho = 0` with every `e_rho` odd forces `SUM n_rho` even, so the number of odd
`n_rho` is even. ∎

Verified: 0 violations across all ten test tuples (all 256 `w` each). Combined with
`K = 0` on `|supp| <= 1`, only **63** of the 256 `w` are live: 18 with `|supp| = 2`, 24
with `|supp| = 3`, 21 with `|supp| = 4`.

## 3. The pair terms, in closed form  `[PROVED]`

Let `supp(w) = {i,j}`, `g = gcd(e_i,e_j)`, `a = e_i/g`, `b = e_j/g` (coprime, both odd).
The resonances with that support are exactly `n_i = kb`, `n_j = -ka`, `k != 0`.

> **Theorem 3.**
> (i) If exactly one of `w_i, w_j` equals `1` then `I(w) = 0`.
> (ii) Otherwise `|I(w)| <= 1/(ab) = g^2/(e_i e_j)`.

*Proof.* (i) `w = 1` forces `n = 2 (mod 4)` and `w in {2,3}` forces `n` odd; with `a,b`
odd this needs `k` simultaneously even and odd. (ii) If `w_i = w_j = 1` then `k = 2 (mod
4)` and `SUM_{k = 2 (4)} k^-2 = pi^2/16`, giving `(16/(pi^2 ab))(pi^2/16) = 1/(ab)`. If
both lie in `{2,3}` then `k` is odd and `SUM_{k odd} k^-2 = pi^2/4`, giving
`(4/(pi^2 ab))(pi^2/4) = 1/(ab)`. ∎

The bound is **sharp**: the observed ratio `|I(w)| / bound` reaches exactly `1`.

> **Corollary 3.1 (the requested bound, for the pair part).** If every pair `(i,j)` has
> `e_i e_j / gcd(e_i,e_j)^2 > R`, then the total `|supp| = 2` contribution to
> `|o − 3/32|` is at most `(144/256)/R = 9/(16 R)`.

This is an inequality of exactly the shape the task asked for, with `C = 9/16` explicit
and `H(e) = min_{i<j} lcm(e_i,e_j)/gcd(e_i,e_j)` an explicit height. It controls the pair
terms only.

## 4. Low-height resonances of full rank force small speeds  `[PROVED]`

For `R >= 1` let `L(e) = { n in Z^4 : n . e = 0 }` (rank 3, saturated) and let `L_R(e)` be
the subgroup generated by `{ n in L(e) : ||n||_inf <= R }`.

> **Theorem 4.** If `rank L_R(e) = 3` and `e` is primitive then
> `max_rho e_rho <= 3 sqrt(3) R^3`.

*Proof.* Take three independent `n^(1),n^(2),n^(3)` in `L_R` and let `c` be the vector of
signed `3x3` minors of the `3x4` matrix they form. Then `c != 0` and `n^(i) . c = 0` for
each `i`, so `c` spans the same line as `e`; primitivity gives `e = +- c/content(c)`.
Hadamard bounds each minor by the product of the row `2`-norms, `<= (R sqrt3)^3`. ∎

Numerically: `41.6` at `R = 2`, `140.3` at `R = 3`, `332.6` at `R = 4`. **Measured** (over
the whole box `max e_rho <= 200`): at `R = 2` exactly **6** tuples have `rank L_2 = 3`,
with maximum speed `15`; at `R = 3`, **665** tuples, maximum speed `95`. The Hadamard
bound is not tight but it is explicit and that is all the reduction needs.

## 5. The class limits are exact rationals, and all are `<= 1/4`  `[PROVED per class; enumeration exhaustive to height 3]`

For a saturated sublattice `L' <= Z^4` let `T_{L'} = { v in T^4 : n . v = 0 for all n in
L' }` and let `o_inf(L')` be the Haar average of the good indicator over `T_{L'}`. Because
`INT_{T_{L'}} e(n . v) dv = 1[n in L']`, `o_inf(L')` is exactly the sub-sum of Theorem 1's
identity over `L'`. Two exact evaluations (`rt_limit_v2.py`):

* **rank 1**, any primitive `n0`. Dropping the coordinate `sigma` gives a surjective
  `|n0_sigma|`-to-1 map `T_{n0} -> T^3` carrying Haar to Haar, so `a_sigma` is the floor of
  a single affine form in three uniforms and its law is the exact Irwin-Hall rational.
* **rank 2**, any saturated `L'`. A column-HNF unimodular transform gives an integer
  `4 x 2` basis `B` of the saturated kernel, so `T_{L'} = { By }` and the four floors
  `floor(B_rho . s)`, `s` uniform on `[0,1)^2`, partition the unit square into convex
  polygons with rational vertices. Areas are exact (clipping + shoelace).

Self-checks: the general rank-1 routine agrees with the `+-1`-entry formula on all 180
admissible vectors of height `<= 3` (0 mismatches); every polygon partition sums to area
`1` exactly; and the exact `o` of deep class members converges to the class limit, e.g.
in the class `((1,0,-2,-1),(0,1,-1,-2))` with `o_inf = 1/4`:
`(17,19,5,7) -> 0.272446`, `(33,27,13,7) -> 0.257446`, `(41,43,13,15) -> 0.253926`.

> **Theorem 5 (enumeration).** Over every resonance class generated by resonances of
> sup-norm height `<= 3`:
>
> | rank of `L'` | realisable classes | maximum `o_inf` | attained at |
> |---|---|---|---|
> | 0 | 1 | `3/32 = 0.09375` | — |
> | 1 | 107 | `1/6 = 0.166667` | the two `+-1` relations, and only those |
> | 2 | 1795 | `1/4 = 0.250000` | 8 classes at height `<= 2` |
>
> **No class has `o_inf > 1/3`; the maximum over all of them is `1/4`.**

At height `<= 2` the counts are 23 rank-1 and 85 rank-2 classes, with the same two maxima.
Both known exceptions lie in rank-2 classes with `o_inf = 1/4` — the top of the table.

**Pushing the height (`rt_limit_probe_v1.py`).** The rank-1 side extends exhaustively:
over **all 612** realisable rank-1 classes of height `<= 5` the maximum is still `1/6`,
attained at `(-1,-1,1,1)`. The rank-2 side at height `<= 5` was **sampled**, not exhausted:
232 distinct realisable classes, maximum `1/4`, **0** classes above `1/4` and **0** above

## 6. The reduction  `[PROVED]`

> **Theorem 6.** Fix `R >= 1`. For every primitive unit 4-tuple `e`, exactly one of:
> * `rank L_R(e) = 3`, and then `max_rho e_rho <= 3 sqrt(3) R^3` (Thm 4) — a finite set; or
> * `rank L_R(e) <= 2`, and then, with `M = L_R(e)` saturated,
>   ```
>       o(e) = o_inf(M) + Tail_R(e),
>       Tail_R(e) = (1/256) SUM_w K(w) eps(w) SUM_{n in L(e) \ M, supp n = supp w} PROD f^(n_rho),
>   ```
>   and **every** `n` occurring in `Tail_R` has `||n||_inf > R`.
>
> Consequently (H'') follows from the finite check on the first arm together with
> ```
>   (H3)      Tail_R(e)  <=  1/3 − o_inf(M)      for every e with rank L_R(e) <= 2.
> ```
> By Theorem 5 the right-hand side is at least `1/3 − 1/4 = 1/12` for `R <= 3`, so at
> `R = 3` the **uniform** sufficient condition
> ```
>   (H3u)     Tail_3(e)  <=  1/12            for every e with rank L_3(e) <= 2
> ```
> already implies (H''), with no class bookkeeping at all.

The splitting is exact — it is a partition of the index set of a convergent absolutely
summable expansion, not an approximation.

**`R = 3` is the operative choice.** At `R = 2` the finite arm is smaller (six tuples,
§7) but (H3u) is *false* — §8 exhibits 16 violations. At `R = 3` the finite arm grows to
665 tuples, all with speed `<= 95`, and (H3u) holds throughout the box measured. So the
statement to aim at is:

> **(H'') follows from (H3u) alone**, the finite arm having been executed in §7.

## 9. Why the requested `C/H(e)` shape cannot carry the whole deviation

Write `T_S = SUM_{n in L, supp n = S} PROD_{rho in S} 1/|n_rho|`, the absolute resonance
sum that bounds `|I(w)|` up to `(4/pi)^{|S|}`. Three facts:

1. `T_S` **converges** for every `S`. Indeed `T_S = INT_0^1 PROD_{rho in S} phi(e_rho
   theta) d theta` with `phi(t) = -2 log|2 sin(pi t)|`, whose Fourier coefficients are
   `1/|n|`; Hölder gives `T_S <= ||phi||_{|S|}^{|S|}`, and
   `INT_0^1 (2 log|2 sin pi t|)^4 dt = 19 pi^4/15 = 123.3848486`, confirmed numerically to
   `3e-9`. (The `|S| = 2` case is `pi^2/3 = 3.2898681`, likewise confirmed.) This is a
   remark establishing that the sums are finite; nothing below rests on the constant, and
   the interchange of sum and integral is stated formally, not proved here.
2. `T_S` **does not decay in the speeds**. If `e` carries any resonance `n` supported on
   `S` with all `|n_rho|` bounded — the `+-1` relations are the extreme case — then
   `T_S >= PROD 1/|n_rho|` is bounded below independently of how large the speeds are.
   Family A has members with arbitrarily large speeds and `T_{1357} >= 1`.
3. Therefore no bound `|o − o_inf| <= C/H(e)` with `H` a function of the speeds alone can
   hold: `o_inf` itself depends on which resonances are present, and the deviation is
   governed by the resonances *outside* the class, not by the size of `e`.

The correct height is not a function of `e` but of the pair `(e, class)`: it is the
sup-norm height `R` of the cut that defines the class. Theorem 3 realises this for the
pair terms — `9/(16R)` — and (H3) is what remains for `|S| = 3` and `|S| = 4`.

**The named gap, stated as one inequality.** Prove

```
   (1/256) * SUM_{w != 0} K(w) eps(w) * SUM_{n in L(e) \ M, supp n = supp w}
                                        PROD_rho f_{w_rho}^(n_rho)     <=  1/12
```

whenever `M = L_3(e)^sat` has rank `<= 2` — this is (H3u). The structural inputs
available are: every `n` in the sum has `||n||_inf > 3`, and no three independent
resonances of height `<= 3` exist (Thm 4's contrapositive), so the third successive
sup-norm minimum of `L(e)` exceeds `3`. The live coefficient sums are `SUM |K| = 144, 192,
216` on `|supp| = 2, 3, 4`, with `(4/pi)^2 = 1.6211`, `(4/pi)^3 = 2.0645`,
`(4/pi)^4 = 2.6288`.

### 9a. The triangle inequality cannot close it  `[PROVED for the pair part; EMPIRICAL for the ratio]`

The first thing to try is `|Tail_R| <= ABS := (1/256) SUM_{w != 0} |K(w)| |I(w)|`. It does
not work, for a reason that can be stated in numbers (`rt_absbound_v1.py`, exact):

| `e` | `o` | `|o − 3/32|` | `ABS` | `ABS` on `|supp|=2` | on 3 | on 4 | `ABS/dev` |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `(1,3,5,7)` | 0.457143 | 0.363393 | 0.538393 | 0.076786 | 0.227381 | 0.234226 | 1.48 |
| `(1,11,5,7)` | 0.337662 | 0.243912 | 0.425731 | 0.046266 | 0.151948 | 0.227516 | 1.75 |
| `(17,3,5,7)` | 0.312605 | 0.218855 | 0.240144 | 0.017122 | 0.132073 | 0.090949 | 1.10 |
| `(1,3,13,15)` | 0.297436 | 0.203686 | 0.390224 | 0.066346 | 0.069231 | 0.254647 | 1.92 |
| `(41,43,13,15)` | 0.253926 | 0.160176 | 0.245282 | 0.001175 | 0.086356 | 0.157751 | 1.53 |
| `(241,163,205,199)` | 0.169378 | 0.075628 | 0.160272 | 0.000014 | 0.003642 | 0.156616 | 2.12 |

Two readings. First, cancellation among the 63 live terms is real but mild — `ABS` exceeds
the true deviation by a factor between `1.1` and `2.1` — so a proof cannot recover much by
being clever about signs at this level. Second, `ABS` runs from `0.0968` to `0.5384` on
these tuples, that is `1.2x` to `6.5x` the `1/12 = 0.0833` budget, and the `|supp| = 4`
block carries most of it even for a tuple whose pair block has collapsed to `1.4e-5`.
(`ABS` bounds the deviation from `3/32`, not `Tail_R`, so this comparison is indicative;
the rigorous statement is the pair-block one that follows, which is measured against the
same budget as `Tail_R`.)

The pair block alone settles the matter, and this part is proved. Every pair resonance is
all-or-nothing: the primitive pair vector `(b, -a)` has height `max(a,b)`, so either it and
all its multiples lie in `M`, or none does and `ab >= max(a,b) >= R+1`. Hence the pair
block of `Tail_R` is at most `9/(16(R+1))`, and:

| `R` | crude pair bound `9/(16(R+1))` | finite-check speed box `3 sqrt3 R^3` |
|---|---|---|
| 2 | 0.187500 | 41.6 |
| 3 | 0.140625 | 140.3 |
| 4 | 0.112500 | 332.6 |
| 7 | 0.070312 | 1782.3 |
| 10 | 0.051136 | 5196.2 |
| 20 | 0.026786 | 41569.2 |

> **Consequence.** At `R = 3` the crude pair bound is `0.1406`, already `1.7x` the whole
> `1/12` budget, so (H3u) cannot be proved by bounding `|I(w)|` termwise at that height.
> The pair bound first drops below `1/12` at `R = 7`, where it consumes `84%` of the
> budget and leaves `0.0130` for the 45 triple and quadruple terms — which the table above
> shows are the dominant block. Meanwhile the finite check has grown to speeds `<= 1782`,
> about `2.5e9` primitive tuples, beyond this engine.

So the triangle-inequality route is closed in both directions: it is too weak at small `R`
and the finite arm is too large at the `R` where it would become strong enough. **A proof
of (H3u) has to use cancellation inside the `|supp| = 3` and `|supp| = 4` blocks**, not
merely bound them. That is the precise obstruction this report leaves.

## 10. The pre-registered sweep

Pre-registration was produced before any of the R=3/R=5 data by the `experiment-designer`
gate; the binding parts are reproduced here.

* **Statistic and decision rule.** `o = Fraction(p,q)`, flag iff `3p > q`; both this and
  `o > Fraction(1,3)` are asserted equal at every evaluation. No float in the decision path.
* **Targeting, stated in advance.** The danger zone is *not* "resonant tuples" — the `+-1`
  families have `o_inf = 1/6`. It is the rank-2 classes at the top of the Theorem 5 table,
  where `o_inf = 1/4`; and both known exceptions lie there. Those classes are
  2-parameter, so they can be exhausted to large speeds cheaply.
* **Controls that must fire.** C1 the enumerator returns `o(1,3,5,7) = 16/35` and
  `o(1,11,5,7) = 26/77`; C2 a deterministic planted height-`R` resonance is recovered with
  a witness tuple; C3 the comparator re-run at `t = 3/10` returns a non-empty flagged set,
  every member independently re-verified by `ob_core_v1.o_x`; C4 a matched non-resonant
  control arm, 1:1 on max speed, seeded.
* **Falsification.** Any `e` outside `{(1,3,5,7),(1,11,5,7)}` with `o > 1/3`, confirmed by
  two independent engines returning the identical `Fraction` and a clean boundary audit.
* **Near-miss reporting is mandatory** alongside any null: max `o` with argmax, and
  `#{o >= 1/3 − d}` for `d = 0, 1/100, 1/50, 1/30, 1/20`.

**Result at `R = 3`, `SMAX2 = 1500`, `SMAX1 = 240`, seed `20260909`.**

* Class enumeration inside the sweep: 1795 realisable rank-2 classes of height `<= 3`,
  maximum limit `1/4`, **8** classes attaining it. Those 8 are the T1 arm.
* **C1 fires** (`16/35` and `26/77` returned exactly). **C2 fires** (planted resonance
  `(-3,-3,-2,2)`, witness `(9,3,5,23)` recovered). **C3 fires** (4 tuples flagged at
  `t = 3/10`, `4/4` re-verified by `ob_core_v1.o_x` to the identical `Fraction`).
* **T1** — every member of the 8 top classes with speeds `<= 1500`, **57 018** tuples:
  `N_exc = 0`. Max `16/35` at `(1,3,5,7)`. Near-miss counts `[2, 2, 2, 2, 6]`. Third-place
  `974/3289 = 0.2961386` at `(1,11,13,23)`.
* **T2** — the two `+-1` families, **exhaustive over speed** `<= 240`, **34 235** tuples:
  `N_exc = 0`. Max `16/35`. Near-miss counts `[2, 2, 2, 2, 7]`. This is the arm that
  repairs the coverage defect of §11(a): 34 235 speed-exhaustive members against the 3409
  parameter-box members of the earlier sweep.
* **C4** — matched non-resonant control, **39 706** tuples, 1:1 on max speed with **0**
  inexact matches, seeded, subsample rate `0.4383` of the 91 253-tuple treatment set:
  `N_exc = 0`, max `o = 99624/647795 = 0.1537894` at `(129,131,5,23)`, near-miss counts
  `[0, 0, 0, 0, 0]`.
* Boundary audits: `0` failures on the T1 top-20, on every T1 tuple within `1/20` of
  `1/3`, on a seeded random sample of 200 T1 tuples, and on the T2 top-20.

**Enrichment.** The treatment arms place 6 and 7 tuples within `1/20` of `1/3`; the matched
control arm places none, and its maximum `0.1538` falls below the treatment arms' twentieth
order statistic. The targeting rationale is borne out on its own pre-declared threshold.

A `R = 2`, `SMAX2 = 400`, `SMAX1 = 120` calibration run gave the same verdict: all controls
fire, `N_exc = 0` on all three arms, treatment near-miss counts `[2,2,2,2,6]` and
`[2,2,2,2,7]`, control `[0,0,0,0,0]` with maximum `65908/359513 = 0.1833258`.

**Scope of this null, verbatim as pre-registered.** Over the enumerated sets — the 57 018
members of the 8 height-`<= 3` rank-2 classes of limit `1/4` with speeds `<= 1500`, the
34 235 primitive unit 4-tuples with all speeds `<= 240` on `e_1+e_7 = e_3+e_5` or
`e_1+e_3 = e_5+e_7`, and the 385 211 primitive unit 4-tuples with speeds `<= 200` of §7 —
evaluated exactly by `o_fast` with boundary audit clean on the stated subsamples, no tuple
other than `(1,3,5,7)` and `(1,11,5,7)` has `o > 1/3`, the observed maximum outside those
two is `186/595 = 0.3126050`, and 8 tuples lie within `1/20` of `1/3`. This says nothing
about tuples outside those sets, and in particular nothing about a counterexample carrying
no resonance of height `<= 3`.

## 11. One coverage defect, and one unresolved number

Per the standing rule, a defect claim quotes the defective sentence with file and section.

**(a) `docs/n8/O_RESONANCE_v1.md` §4, table row (line 71):**

> `| **resonant families A and B**, speeds ≤ 295 | 3409 | A: `16/35`, B: `26/77` | `(1,3,5,7)`, `(1,11,5,7)` | **0** |`

The producing script `experiments/n8/hp_reshunt_v1.py` enumerates a **parameter box**
`a,b,c <= CAP` with `e = (8a+1, 8b+3, 8c+5, 8d+7)`, not a speed box. Recomputed with that
script's own filters (`len(set(e)) == 4`, `gcd = 1`): `CAP = 12` reproduces the count
**3409 exactly**, and the maximum speed reachable in it is **199**, not 295. (`CAP = 14`
gives 5245 and maximum speed 231.) The set of primitive unit 4-tuples with *all* speeds
`<= 295` lying on either relation has **64 256** members, so the row covers **5.3%** of
what its label states. The two maxima quoted in the row are unaffected — both are attained
at speed `<= 11`. §7 and §10 above supersede the row: a speed-exhaustive census of the
whole box to 200 (385 211 tuples) and a speed-exhaustive family census to 240 (34 235
tuples).

*A related claim I checked and withdrew.* I first read the neighbouring row
`| exhaustive, all tuples, speeds ≤ 71 | 6561 |` as a primitivity error, because the
primitive count at speeds `<= 71` is 6470. It is not an error: that row says "all tuples",
and 6561 is the correct count of unit 4-tuples with every speed `<= 71`. What the two rows
do is use different populations without saying so — the next row's 5544 is the
**primitive** non-resonant count, the all-tuples non-resonant count being 5592. That is a
labelling inconsistency, not a wrong number, and the row's own conclusion (`0` tuples above
`1/3`) is unaffected.

**(b) `docs/n8/O_RESONANCE_v1.md` §3, table row:**

> `| the next height class | `65/256 ≈ 0.25391` |`

and §3's lead-in, "`65/256` for the next height class", graded `[DERIVED + numerically
confirmed]` "to 9e-6". The exhaustive class enumeration of Theorem 5 produces **no class
with limit `65/256`**: the rank-1 limits at height `<= 3` are `1/6, 1/8, 5/48, 11/108,
19/216, 25/288, 1/12, 7/96, 1/24` and the rank-2 maximum is `1/4`. `65/256 = 0.2539063`.
I did not reproduce a derivation for it and do not know its provenance; I note only that
`o(41,43,13,15) = 87296/343785 = 0.2539259`, a member of a `o_inf = 1/4` class, agrees with

`O_STRUCTURE_v2.md`'s header is exactly a case of a near-`1/4` value being read as an exact
rational. This is flagged as a **discrepancy to resolve**, not as a settled error: my
enumeration is exhaustive only over classes generated at height `<= 3`, so a class of

from `65/256` survives; only the constant itself is unverified.
